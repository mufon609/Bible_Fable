# Judging variants

Project-wide method and the running table of cases. A row is added whenever witnesses disagree in a way that changes the English. Cases from any book go here, in the order met.

When the witnesses disagree, two kinds of evidence decide, and the Hebrew does not win by default.

**External:** how many independent traditions carry each reading, how old, and in what language. The LXX is the oldest document (3rd c. BCE) but a translation, read through 4th-c. CE Christian manuscripts; its Hebrew source is inferred. The DSS, Samaritan Pentateuch and MT are three Hebrew lines.

**Internal:** which reading explains the other. Scribes smooth, harmonize and add; they rarely roughen or delete. So the harder, shorter, less regular reading is usually original.

| Case | Readings | External | Internal | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Gen 1:7 "and it was so" | MT/DSS/SP: after the execution (v.7). LXX: after the command (v.6). | 3 Hebrew lines vs 1 Greek | MT is irregular (v.9 has it after the command); LXX is uniform. Standardizing produces LXX; nothing produces MT from LXX. | Hebrew |
| Gen 1:8 "God saw that it was good" | MT/DSS/SP: absent on day 2. LXX: present. | 3 vs 1 | Every other day has it; day 3 has it twice (1:10, 1:12). A scribe fills a gap; none creates one. | Hebrew |
| Deut 32:8 "sons of \_\_\_" | MT: "sons of Israel." LXX: "sons of God." DSS 4QDeut-j: "sons of God" (Hebrew). | Greek + 1 Hebrew line vs MT | "Sons of God" dividing the nations is the harder reading; "Israel" removes a divine council. | LXX/DSS, against MT |
| Gen 2:2 "on the \_\_\_ day God finished" | MT: "seventh." LXX, Samaritan Pentateuch, Syriac, Old Latin: "sixth"; Jubilees 2:16 and Josephus (Ant. 1.33) also say the work ended on the sixth. 4QGen-k preserves 2:1–3; whether this word survives on it is unchecked (DJD XII). | 1 Hebrew line vs 1 Hebrew + Greek + Syriac + Latin | "Seventh" has God finishing work on the day he ceases from it; "sixth" removes that. Three independent scribes could each make the same fix; none would introduce the problem. | MT, against three witnesses |
| Gen 2:18 "I will make" / "let us make" | MT, SP: "I will make" (singular). LXX: "let us make." | 2 Hebrew lines vs 1 Greek | The LXX matches the wording of 1:26; a scribe harmonizes toward the famous phrase, not away from it. | Hebrew |
| Gen 2:24 "they" / "the two" | MT: "they become one flesh." LXX, SP, Syriac, Vulgate, and the Gospel quotations: "the two." | 1 Hebrew line vs 1 Hebrew + Greek + Syriac + Latin | The MT is shorter; "the two" clarifies an open "they." Adding a clarifier is the common scribal move; dropping one has no motive. But the Samaritan agreement means a second Hebrew line had "two" early. | MT, with lower confidence than the cases above |
| Gen 3:1 "living-thing of the field" / "on the earth" | MT, SP: "of the field" (ha-sadeh). LXX: "upon the earth." 4QGen-k covers 3:1–2; whether this word survives on it is unchecked (DJD XII). | 2 Hebrew lines vs 1 Greek | "Of the field" is the phrase of 2:19–20 and 3:14; the LXX generalizes. A translator broadens; nothing produces the narrower phrase from the broad one. | Hebrew |
| Gen 3:6 "and he ate" / "and they ate" | MT, SP: singular (vayyokhal). LXX: plural (ephagon). | 2 Hebrew lines vs 1 Greek | The MT has her eat, then give, then him eat: three singulars. The plural sums up both. Summing up is smoothing; no scribe splits a plural into a sequence. | Hebrew |
| Gen 3:15 "he shall strike" / "he shall watch" / "she shall crush" | MT, SP: hu yeshufkha, "he/it shall strike you," the same verb for both blows. LXX: autos terei, "he shall watch" (both times). Vulgate: ipsa conteret, "she shall crush" (first blow only). | 2 Hebrew lines vs Greek (verb) and Latin (pronoun) | The Greek is a rendering of a rare verb (4 uses), not another consonantal text; the Latin reads the consonants הוא as feminine, which the Torah's spelling permits and the Masoretic vowels exclude. Neither is evidence of different Hebrew letters. The pronoun's gender rests on vowels; the verb's sense on Job 9:17. | Hebrew; the pronoun flagged as vowel-dependent |
| Gen 3:16 "your desire" / "your return" | MT: teshuqatekh (תשוקתך), "your desire." SP: teshuvatekh (תשובתך), "your return." LXX: apostrophe, "turning"; Syriac likewise. Vulgate: "you shall be under the power of your husband," a paraphrase. | 1 Hebrew line vs 1 Hebrew + Greek + Syriac | One letter apart (ק/ב). teshuqah is rare (3 uses: 3:16; 4:7; Song 7:11); teshuvah, "return," is common. The rare word is the one a scribe replaces with the common one; 4:7 repeats the same rare word in the MT with the same structure, and the SP reads "return" there too, which shows the change made consistently. The common reading does not explain the rare one. | MT, against three witnesses |
| Gen 3:17 "on account of you" / "in your works" | MT, SP: ba'avurekha (בעבורך), "on account of you." LXX: en tois ergois sou, "in your works," as if בעבדתך. Vulgate follows the Hebrew (in opere tuo is a loose rendering of the same). | 2 Hebrew lines vs 1 Greek | ר and ד are easily confused in the square script; either reading could arise from the other by one letter. The Hebrew lines agree; the Greek reading fits its own next clause ("in toil you shall eat") and may be a smoothing toward it. | Hebrew, with lower confidence |
| Gen 3:24 object of "stationed" | MT, SP: "he stationed to the east of the garden the cherubim," no "him." LXX: "he settled him opposite the garden of delight, and set the cherubim," adding a pronoun and a second verb. | 2 Hebrew lines vs 1 Greek | The LXX supplies an object ("him") and a verb the Hebrew lacks, and so answers where the human went. Supplying is the common move; the Hebrew leaves the human's destination unstated beyond "the ground" (3:23). The shorter reading stands. | Hebrew |

The third case shows the method cutting against the Masoretic Text; each variant is judged on its own.
