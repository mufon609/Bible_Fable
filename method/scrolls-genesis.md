# Scroll table for Genesis

Built 2026-10-24 in a dedicated session (no translation work). One row per Dead Sea Scroll fragment listed as containing any part of Genesis. The table replaces the Genesis entry of the witness table in `method/method.md` as the place where a scroll's extent, script, date and edition are recorded; the witness table points here. Under the scroll rule in CLAUDE.md, a scroll is cited in a note, variant row or finding only for a verse whose extent has been confirmed in DJD, volume and page named. The grades below say how far each range got toward that rule on 2026-10-24.

## Grades

- **A**: a plate was opened at deadseascrolls.org.il in a browser, its image ID is recorded, and letters of the range were read on it.
- **B**: the range is confirmed in Tov, *Revised Lists* (2010), or in Ulrich, *The Biblical Qumran Scrolls* (2010), which prints the DJD transcriptions.
- **C**: the range rests on a secondary list only (the Wikipedia lists, dssenglishbible.com).

A and B both satisfy the scroll rule's "confirmed" requirement only in part: the DJD page itself has still not been opened for any scroll. Ulrich's transcriptions reproduce the DJD editions (his Genesis section is "based on editions by Baillet, Barthélemy, Davila, Puech, Skehan–Ulrich, Tigchelaar"), and the DJD volume and pages in the table are taken from the IAA manuscript pages and from Tov; the two agree wherever both give them. A B-grade range may therefore be cited with the DJD reference the table gives, and the note should say "DJD XII, pp. 31–38, as reproduced in Ulrich, BQS, p. 7" until DJD itself is opened.

## Sources reached

1. **deadseascrolls.org.il** (Leon Levy Dead Sea Scrolls Digital Library, Israel Antiquities Authority). Reached in a browser; automated requests return the page shell only. The manuscript page of every Qumran Genesis scroll listed below was opened and gives site, script, period, material, the list of plate images (B-number, PAM number, date, photographer) and a "Publications" line naming the DJD volume and pages. The non-Qumran fragments are entered under their own sigla (MUR1, Mas 1, WS1) and were found through the site's search. Plates were opened for every scroll covering Gen 1–11; the image IDs and what was read are in the table. The site's image viewer zooms to the resolution of the scan: the 2011–2015 Shai Halevi infrared images read easily; the 1950s PAM negatives read at word level only on the larger fragments.
2. **Tov, *Revised Lists of the Texts from the Judaean Desert* (Brill, 2010).** Reached in Google Books snippet view only (books.google.com, id 8O95DwAAQBAJ; "This is a preview. 3. **Ulrich, *The Biblical Qumran Scrolls: Transcriptions and Textual Variants* (VTSup 134, Brill, 2010).** Not previewable on Google Books (the API returned nothing for the volume). The text was read from the OCR of a copy on archive.org (identifiers TheBiblicalQumranScrolls and the-biblical-qumran-scrolls-transcriptions-and-textual-variants; the first OCR is unusable, the second readable in its English headers and in most Hebrew lines). That copy is a community upload, not a publisher's preview, which the director should weigh before treating the B grades that rest on it as settled; no text was copied from it into this repository beyond the fragment headers and single words needed to state what survives. Ulrich's Genesis section occupies pp. 1–26 of the volume; its "Index of Biblical Passages" at the end lists every Genesis verse preserved in any Judaean Desert manuscript with the scroll that preserves it, and that index is the source of the ranges given for the scrolls Ulrich does not transcribe (Murabba'at, Sdeir, Masada, X9–X10). Ulrich's headers give fragment numbers and verse ranges; his apparatus gives the variants.
4. **Secondary lists** (used to find candidates only): the Wikipedia list of the Dead Sea Scrolls, the Wikipedia list of Cave 4 manuscripts, dssenglishbible.com (scroll pages 1Q1, 4Q1, 4Q2, 4Q4, 4Q7, 4Q8, 4Q10, 6Q1), and Tigchelaar's 2023 note on 4Q1 (lirias.kuleuven.be), which quotes Davila's date for 4Q1 and reproduces the DJD XII fragment numbering. DJD I, II, III, VII, IX, XII, XXV, XXXVIII and Masada VI themselves were not opened. DJD III and DJD IX are on archive.org as lending copies (discoveriesinjud0000bail, qumrancave4ivpal0009unse); DJD XII is not there.

## The five questions set for this session

1. **4Q1's Genesis extent.** Ulrich's transcription and Tigchelaar's reproduction of the DJD XII fragment table both begin the scroll's Genesis at frg. 1 = 22:14 and run to 49:8 (frg. 16); Exodus begins at frg. 17. Ulrich's Index of Biblical Passages nevertheless lists "8:20–21? 4QGen-Exod-a" with a query. So the "8:20–21" of the Wikipedia list is a queried entry of the published index, not a slip for Exod 8:20–22; the Genesis 8 session's explanation was wrong, its conclusion (no citable scroll for Gen 8) stands. Which fragment carries the query is to be read in DJD XII, pp. 29–30.
2. **4QGen-b at 4:2–11 and 5:13.** Ulrich prints frg. 3 i as 4:2–11, twelve lines, and frg. 3 ii as "5:13 or 14", a few letters. The 4:2–11 range is the edition's; the Wikipedia list's 4:2–4 is short.
