# Session brief (as kept in the doc)

Kept verbatim from the Genesis Literal Translation Log, migrated 2026-10-24. Where it says "tab," read the file named in CLAUDE.md. CLAUDE.md is the operative version of these instructions.

Standing instructions for any session that continues this work. Read the whole doc first; this tab says what to produce and what not to.

## What the project is

A translation of Genesis from the Hebrew consonantal text, checked against the oldest witnesses, in two layers: a literal text (the evidence) and a readable text (the product), with a one-line correction under any verse where a well-known reading differs from the Hebrew. Study of the text itself. No tradition's, school's or commentator's interpretation is reported, and no opinion is added.

## Tabs and what goes on each

- **Main log**: Method; Judging variants; one section per verse: bold translation line, pointed Hebrew, transliteration, then a table (Hebrew / Transliteration / Literal / Note). Notes state form and usage only. Version comparison tables at the end.
- **Running translation**: the literal text, assembled verse by verse. Bracket supplied words.
- **Readable translation**: the literal text with English grammar allowed to touch it and nothing else; a ⸤ ⸥ line beneath a verse wherever it was rephrased; an italic one-sentence correction with citations under a verse where a well-known reading differs from the text. No version is ever named on this tab.
- **Translation → belief**: one row per Hebrew term whose familiar English rendering permits a reading the Hebrew does not. Columns: Hebrew term, Where, What the Hebrew says, KJV rendering, Reading the English permits, Versions that correct it. Versions compared: KJV, NIV, ESV, NASB, NRSV, JPS; no others.
- **Findings**: numbered conclusions, each stated as a pattern in the vocabulary, with verses and belief-tab rows listed. Weight stated as a count where a parallel is relied on.
- **Concordance**: one entry per term the Findings lean on: total, fixed rendering, distribution by sense with counts, occurrences that do not fit, weight, status.

## Rules (all in Method; repeated here)

1. One English word per Hebrew word where English allows; verb forms, articles, number and repeated structure kept as the Hebrew has them; a word's range settled by Genesis's own usage, never by doctrine.
2. Variants: the Hebrew does not win by default. External evidence (how many independent lines, how old) and internal evidence (the harder, shorter reading usually explains the smoother one). Flag anything that rests on Masoretic vowels or accents.
3. Parallels recorded, conclusions confined to Findings. A note says where else the Bible uses the word and what it means there; it does not say what that means for the verse.
4. Extra-biblical data (cognates, Near Eastern practice) only to fix a word's meaning, never to supply a picture.
5. A parallel carries its full distribution, hits and misses, and its weight as a count. A new term the Findings lean on gets a Concordance entry.
6. Belief tab: state what the English permits; a claim about what readers believed only with a citation.
7. Readable tab: three permitted changes only, (A) English word order or phrasing of a construct, (B) a copula or reciprocal the Hebrew form supplies, (C) a bracketed supplied word unbracketed. Corrections only for well-known misreadings, one or two sentences, with one to four verses a reader can open. "It was" in "saw that it was good" is supplied without marking.
8. Transliteration: *ch* ח, *kh* soft כ, *tz* צ, *q* ק, *sh* שׁ, *s* שׂ ס; *'* for ע; doubled consonants for dagesh forte.

## Witnesses

MT (Leningrad), Samaritan Pentateuch, LXX, Syriac and Vulgate where they differ. DSS attest Genesis only in fragments; cite a scroll only where it covers the verse. For ch. 3: 4QGen-k (3:1–2), 1QGen (3:11–14). The witness table in Method lists all Genesis scrolls with their verses.

## Per-chapter deliverables

1. Main log: a section for every verse in order, appended after the last existing verse section and before "Running translation."
2. Running translation: the chapter appended.
3. Readable translation: the chapter appended, with ⸤ ⸥ lines and corrections.
4. Translation → belief: a row for each new term that qualifies, numbered on from the last row.
5. Findings: confirm, sharpen or complicate the existing six; add a new one only when a pattern appears that the existing six do not cover. Update the coverage line.
6. Concordance: an entry for any term a Finding newly leans on; complete representative lists where the chapter touches them.
7. Judging variants: a row for any variant where witnesses disagree in a way that changes the English.
8. Version comparison: a table for the chapter in the same format as the existing ones.

## Do not

- Rewrite, reorder or restyle anything already in the doc. Append and extend; correct an earlier entry only when the new chapter proves it wrong, and say so in the handoff.
- Name a version, group or commentator on the Readable tab.
- Report what any tradition teaches a verse means.
- Use "genuinely," "honestly," or hedge language; state the form and stop.
- Add a Finding that is a conclusion about what the text intends rather than a pattern in its words.

## Handoff

End the session with a short section on this tab under "Session log": date, chapter covered, rows and entries added (by number), any earlier entry corrected and why, and anything left unchecked. The next session audits from that list.
