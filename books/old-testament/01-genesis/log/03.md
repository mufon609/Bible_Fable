# Genesis 3 — verse log

One section per verse: bold translation line, pointed Hebrew, transliteration, then a table (Hebrew / Transliteration / Literal / Note). Notes state form and usage only. Rules in method/method.md.

## Genesis 3:1

**Translation: And the serpent was shrewd, more than every living-thing of the field which YHWH God had made; and he said to the woman, "Even so, that God said, 'You shall not eat from every tree of the garden'?"**

וְהַנָּחָשׁ הָיָה עָרוּם מִכֹּל חַיַּת הַשָּׂדֶה אֲשֶׁר עָשָׂה יְהוָה אֱלֹהִים וַיֹּאמֶר אֶל־הָאִשָּׁה אַף כִּי־אָמַר אֱלֹהִים לֹא תֹאכְלוּ מִכֹּל עֵץ הַגָּן

*ve-ha-nachash hayah arum mi-kol chayyat ha-sadeh asher asah YHWH Elohim vayyomer el-ha-ishah af ki-amar Elohim lo tokhlu mi-kol etz ha-gan*

| Hebrew | Transliteration | Literal | Note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| וְהַנָּחָשׁ הָיָה | ve-ha-nachash hayah | and the serpent was | ve- + noun + perfect: background, as 1:2 and 2:5. With the article, as if already known. nachash is the ordinary snake word: the staff that becomes a snake (Ex 4:3; 7:15), the biting snakes of the desert and the bronze one (Num 21:6–9), the snake in the wall (Amos 5:19), the snake that bites when a hedge is broken (Eccl 10:8). It is not the tannin of 1:21; Ex 7:9–12 uses tannin for the same staff that Ex 4:3 calls nachash, so the words overlap but are not one. 31 uses; see Concordance. KJV: "serpent." |
| עָרוּם | arum | shrewd | Consonants ערום, the same letters as "naked" in 2:25 (ערומים, plural); the vowels and the context distinguish them. Vowel-dependent as to pointing, not as to sense: a snake is not "naked." arum is the word Proverbs uses for the prudent man (12:16, 23; 13:16; 14:8, 15, 18; 22:3; 27:12) and Job for the crafty (5:12; 15:5). KJV: "subtil," 1611 English for crafty. |
| מִכֹּל חַיַּת הַשָּׂדֶה | mi-kol chayyat ha-sadeh | from every living-thing of the field | min as comparative: "more than." The same phrase as 2:19–20; the serpent is placed among the animals the human named. LXX: "upon the earth" for "of the field." MT and SP: "of the field." 4QGen-k covers this verse; whether the word survives on the fragment is unchecked (DJD XII). Corrected 2026-10-24 from method/scrolls-genesis.md: השדה is not on the fragment. 4QGen-k frg. 5 keeps of 3:1 only ]ערום[ and ויאמ]ר אל האשה האף (DJD XII, pp. 75–78, as reproduced in Ulrich, BQS, p. 7; fragment not located on a plate). The same line reads האף, with the interrogative *he* before the particle, where MT and SP have אף; see Judging variants, Gen 3:1. |
| אֲשֶׁר עָשָׂה יְהוָה אֱלֹהִים | asher asah YHWH Elohim | which YHWH God had made | asah, the ordinary making verb. The serpent is one of the made things; the text states its origin in this clause. |
| אֶל־הָאִשָּׁה | el-ha-ishah | to the woman | The serpent speaks to the woman, who was not present at the command of 2:16–17. |
| אַף כִּי־אָמַר | af ki-amar | even so, that he said | af ki, "even that," "indeed that": elsewhere "how much more" (1 Sam 14:30; 2 Sam 4:11; 16:11), or "even though" (Neh 9:18). Here it opens a question or a half-statement with no main clause; the sentence is left unfinished. KJV: "Yea, hath God said." |
| לֹא תֹאכְלוּ מִכֹּל עֵץ | lo tokhlu mi-kol etz | you shall not eat from every tree | Plural "you"; the command of 2:17 was singular. lo … mi-kol can mean "not from any" or "not from all"; the serpent's wording reverses 2:16, "from every tree you may eat." |

## Genesis 3:2

וַתֹּאמֶר הָאִשָּׁה אֶל־הַנָּחָשׁ מִפְּרִי עֵץ־הַגָּן נֹאכֵל

*vattomer ha-ishah el-ha-nachash mi-peri etz-ha-gan nokhel*

| Hebrew | Transliteration | Literal | Note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| מִפְּרִי עֵץ־הַגָּן | mi-peri etz-ha-gan | from the fruit of the tree of the garden | The woman adds "fruit" (2:16 had "from every tree") and drops "every." |
| נֹאכֵל | nokhel | we may eat | Plural, first person. The stressed "eating you may eat" of 2:16 is not repeated. 4QGen-k covers this verse; its surviving letters are reported as matching the MT; plates not inspected. ## Genesis 3:3

**Translation: but from the fruit of the tree which is in the midst of the garden, God said, 'You shall not eat from it, and you shall not touch it, lest you die.'"**

וּמִפְּרִי הָעֵץ אֲשֶׁר בְּתוֹךְ־הַגָּן אָמַר אֱלֹהִים לֹא תֹאכְלוּ מִמֶּנּוּ וְלֹא תִגְּעוּ בּוֹ פֶּן־תְּמֻתוּן

*u-mi-peri ha-etz asher betokh-ha-gan amar Elohim lo tokhlu mimmennu ve-lo tigge'u bo pen-temutun*

| Hebrew | Transliteration | Literal | Note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| הָעֵץ אֲשֶׁר בְּתוֹךְ־הַגָּן | ha-etz asher betokh-ha-gan | the tree which is in the midst of the garden | In 2:9 "in the midst of the garden" was attached to the tree of life; the woman uses it of the forbidden tree, which she does not name. |
| אָמַר אֱלֹהִים | amar Elohim | God said | Elohim alone, as in the serpent's mouth (3:1, 5). The narrator of ch. 2–3 says "YHWH God" throughout; the two speakers in the dialogue say only "God." |
| וְלֹא תִגְּעוּ בּוֹ | ve-lo tigge'u bo | and you shall not touch it | naga, to touch. Not in the command of 2:17. |
| פֶּן־תְּמֻתוּן | pen-temutun | lest you die | pen, "lest." The stressed form mot tamut of 2:17 is not repeated; the long ending -un is an older or emphatic plural form. |

## Genesis 3:4

**Translation: And the serpent said to the woman, "Not dying you shall die;**

וַיֹּאמֶר הַנָּחָשׁ אֶל־הָאִשָּׁה לֹא־מוֹת תְּמֻתוּן

*vayyomer ha-nachash el-ha-ishah lo-mot temutun*

| Hebrew | Transliteration | Literal | Note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| לֹא־מוֹת תְּמֻתוּן | lo-mot temutun | not dying you shall die | The stressed form of 2:17 (mot tamut) with a negative in front of it and a plural ending. Hebrew normally places the negative between infinitive and verb (mot lo tamut); putting lo before the whole pair negates the sentence as pronounced: the death sentence of 2:17, denied. The serpent, not the woman, quotes the form of 2:17. KJV: "Ye shall not surely die." |

## Genesis 3:5

**Translation: for God knows that in the day of your eating from it, your eyes will be opened and you will be like God, knowers of good and bad."**

כִּי יֹדֵעַ אֱלֹהִים כִּי בְּיוֹם אֲכָלְכֶם מִמֶּנּוּ וְנִפְקְחוּ עֵינֵיכֶם וִהְיִיתֶם כֵּאלֹהִים יֹדְעֵי טוֹב וָרָע

*ki yodea Elohim ki be-yom akholkhem mimmennu ve-nifqechu eineikhem vi-hyitem ke-Elohim yod'ei tov va-ra*

| Hebrew | Transliteration | Literal | Note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| בְּיוֹם אֲכָלְכֶם | be-yom akholkhem | in the day of your eating | The phrase of 2:17 with a plural suffix. |
| וְנִפְקְחוּ עֵינֵיכֶם | ve-nifqechu eineikhem | and your eyes will be opened | paqach: to open eyes or ears (Gen 21:19; 2 Kgs 6:17; Isa 35:5). Fulfilled in the same words in 3:7. |
| כֵּאלֹהִים | ke-Elohim | like God | The same word rendered "God" throughout the log. The form is plural and takes singular or plural sense by context; no vowel or consonant settles it here. LXX: "like gods" (theoi); Vulgate: "like gods" (dii). The phrase "like one of us" in 3:22 is the narrator's counterpart. KJV: "as gods." |
| יֹדְעֵי טוֹב וָרָע | yod'ei tov va-ra | knowers of good and bad | Plural participle agreeing with "you," not with Elohim. The phrase of 2:9 and 2:17 as a participle: ones who know. |

## Genesis 3:6

**Translation: And the woman saw that the tree was good for food, and that it was a desire to the eyes, and the tree desirable to make wise; and she took from its fruit and ate; and she gave also to her man with her, and he ate.**

וַתֵּרֶא הָאִשָּׁה כִּי טוֹב הָעֵץ לְמַאֲכָל וְכִי תַאֲוָה־הוּא לָעֵינַיִם וְנֶחְמָד הָעֵץ לְהַשְׂכִּיל וַתִּקַּח מִפִּרְיוֹ וַתֹּאכַל וַתִּתֵּן גַּם־לְאִישָׁהּ עִמָּהּ וַיֹּאכַל

*vattere ha-ishah ki tov ha-etz le-ma'akhal ve-khi ta'avah-hu la-einayim ve-nechmad ha-etz le-haskil vattiqqach mi-piryo vattokhal vattitten gam-le-ishah immah vayyokhal*

| Hebrew | Transliteration | Literal | Note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| וַתֵּרֶא הָאִשָּׁה כִּי טוֹב | vattere ha-ishah ki tov | and the woman saw that good | The formula of ch. 1 ("and God saw that good") with the woman as subject. |
| תַאֲוָה־הוּא לָעֵינַיִם | ta'avah-hu la-einayim | a desire it \[is\] to the eyes | ta'avah, desire, craving (Num 11:4, the craving for meat; Ps 10:3). |
| וְנֶחְמָד הָעֵץ לְהַשְׂכִּיל | ve-nechmad ha-etz le-haskil | and the tree desirable to make wise | nechmad, the word of 2:9 for every tree in the garden. sakhal (hiphil): to have insight, act wisely, or to look at (Gen 48:14, "guiding" his hands). LXX: "to look upon"; Vulgate: "to look upon." KJV: "to make one wise." |
| וַתִּקַּח מִפִּרְיוֹ וַתֹּאכַל | vattiqqach mi-piryo vattokhal | and she took from its fruit and ate | peri, fruit; no species is named here or anywhere in the chapter. |
| גַּם־לְאִישָׁהּ עִמָּהּ | gam-le-ishah immah | also to her man with her | ish with the suffix "her": her man. "With her" places him present at the eating; the text does not say he was present for the conversation. KJV: "unto her husband with her." |
| וַיֹּאכַל | vayyokhal | and he ate | Singular. Variant: LXX "and they ate" (ephagon). MT and SP: singular. Added to Judging variants. |

## Genesis 3:7

**Translation: And the eyes of the two of them were opened, and they knew that they were naked; and they sewed fig leaf and made for themselves belts.**

וַתִּפָּקַחְנָה עֵינֵי שְׁנֵיהֶם וַיֵּדְעוּ כִּי עֵירֻמִּם הֵם וַיִּתְפְּרוּ עֲלֵה תְאֵנָה וַיַּעֲשׂוּ לָהֶם חֲגֹרֹת

*vattippaqachnah einei sheneihem vayyed'u ki eirummim hem vayyitperu aleh te'enah vayya'asu lahem chagorot*

| Hebrew | Transliteration | Literal | Note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| וַתִּפָּקַחְנָה עֵינֵי שְׁנֵיהֶם | vattippaqachnah einei sheneihem | and the eyes of the two of them were opened | The serpent's words of 3:5 carried out. "The two of them" as in 2:25. |
| וַיֵּדְעוּ | vayyed'u | and they knew | yada, the verb behind da'at (2:9, 17) and yod'ei (3:5). What they know is their nakedness. |
| עֵירֻמִּם | eirummim | naked | Spelled with a yod (עירמם), unlike 2:25 (ערומים). Same word, different spelling; the consonants here cannot be read as "shrewd." The same spelling in 3:10, 11. |
| עֲלֵה תְאֵנָה | aleh te'enah | fig leaf | Singular collective: fig foliage. The fig is the one tree named in the chapter. |
| חֲגֹרֹת | chagorot | belts | chagor, a girdle or belt worn at the waist (1 Sam 18:4; 2 Sam 18:11; 1 Kgs 2:5; Isa 3:24). KJV: "aprons." |

## Genesis 3:8

**Translation: And they heard the sound of YHWH God walking about in the garden at the wind of the day; and the human and his woman hid themselves from the face of YHWH God in the midst of the tree of the garden.**

וַיִּשְׁמְעוּ אֶת־קוֹל יְהוָה אֱלֹהִים מִתְהַלֵּךְ בַּגָּן לְרוּחַ הַיּוֹם וַיִּתְחַבֵּא הָאָדָם וְאִשְׁתּוֹ מִפְּנֵי יְהוָה אֱלֹהִים בְּתוֹךְ עֵץ הַגָּן

*vayyishme'u et-qol YHWH Elohim mithallekh ba-gan le-ruach ha-yom vayyitchabbe ha-adam ve-ishto mi-penei YHWH Elohim betokh etz ha-gan*

| Hebrew | Transliteration | Literal | Note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| קוֹל | qol | sound | Voice or sound; of thunder (Ex 19:16), of footsteps (2 Sam 5:24), of a voice (3:10, 17). KJV: "voice." |
| מִתְהַלֵּךְ בַּגָּן | mithallekh ba-gan | walking about in the garden | hithpael of halakh: to walk to and fro (Gen 5:22, 24, Enoch walked with God; Deut 23:15, God walking in the camp; 2 Sam 7:6, God walking about in a tent). The participle agrees with YHWH God, not with the sound. |
| לְרוּחַ הַיּוֹם | le-ruach ha-yom | at the wind of the day | ruach, the word of 1:2. The phrase occurs only here. LXX: "in the afternoon"; Vulgate: "at the breeze after midday"; both are readings of the phrase, not other words. KJV: "in the cool of the day." |
| וַיִּתְחַבֵּא הָאָדָם וְאִשְׁתּוֹ | vayyitchabbe ha-adam ve-ishto | and the human and his woman hid themselves | Singular verb before two subjects, ordinary Hebrew. "The human," with the article, as in 2:25. |
| בְּתוֹךְ עֵץ הַגָּן | betokh etz ha-gan | in the midst of the tree of the garden | Singular "tree" used collectively, as in 2:16. |

| --- | --- | --- | --- |

## Genesis 3:10

וַיֹּאמֶר אֶת־קֹלְךָ שָׁמַעְתִּי בַּגָּן וָאִירָא כִּי־עֵירֹם אָנֹכִי וָאֵחָבֵא

*vayyomer et-qolkha shama'ti ba-gan va-ira ki-eirom anokhi va-echave*

| Hebrew | Transliteration | Literal | Note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| אֶת־קֹלְךָ שָׁמַעְתִּי | et-qolkha shama'ti | your sound I heard | Object first, for emphasis. qol as in 3:8. |

## Genesis 3:12

**Translation: And the human said, "The woman whom you gave with me, she gave to me from the tree, and I ate."**

וַיֹּאמֶר הָאָדָם הָאִשָּׁה אֲשֶׁר נָתַתָּה עִמָּדִי הִוא נָתְנָה־לִּי מִן־הָעֵץ וָאֹכֵל

*vayyomer ha-adam ha-ishah asher natattah immadi hi natenah-li min-ha-etz va-okhel*

| Hebrew | Transliteration | Literal | Note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| אֲשֶׁר נָתַתָּה עִמָּדִי | asher natattah immadi | whom you gave with me | natan, to give: the human's word for 2:22's "brought." |

## Genesis 3:13

**Translation: And YHWH God said to the woman, "What is this you have done?" And the woman said, "The serpent deceived me, and I ate."**

וַיֹּאמֶר יְהוָה אֱלֹהִים לָאִשָּׁה מַה־זֹּאת עָשִׂית וַתֹּאמֶר הָאִשָּׁה הַנָּחָשׁ הִשִּׁיאַנִי וָאֹכֵל

*vayyomer YHWH Elohim la-ishah mah-zot asit vattomer ha-ishah ha-nachash hishshi'ani va-okhel*

| Hebrew | Transliteration | Literal | Note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| מַה־זֹּאת עָשִׂית | mah-zot asit | what \[is\] this you have done | The question put to the woman; the serpent is not questioned. |
| הִשִּׁיאַנִי | hishshi'ani | deceived me | nasha (hiphil): to lead astray, deceive (2 Kgs 18:29; Jer 4:10; 29:8; 37:9; Obad 3, 7). KJV: "beguiled." |

## Genesis 3:14

## Genesis 3:16

| Hebrew | Transliteration | Literal | Note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| אֶל־הָאִשָּׁה אָמַר | el-ha-ishah amar | to the woman he said | No "cursed" in this speech. |

| עִצְּבוֹנֵךְ וְהֵרֹנֵךְ | itzevonekh ve-heronekh | your toil and your pregnancy | itzavon: toil, hard labor, pain. 3 uses: here, 3:17 (the man's eating), 5:29 (the toil of our hands). **Translation: And he drove out the human, and he stationed from east of the garden of Eden the cherubim and the flame of the turning sword, to keep the way of the tree of life.**

וַיְגָרֶשׁ אֶת־הָאָדָם וַיַּשְׁכֵּן מִקֶּדֶם לְגַן־עֵדֶן אֶת־הַכְּרֻבִים וְאֵת לַהַט הַחֶרֶב הַמִּתְהַפֶּכֶת לִשְׁמֹר אֶת־דֶּרֶךְ עֵץ הַחַיִּים

*vaygaresh et-ha-adam vayyashken miqqedem le-gan-Eden et-ha-keruvim ve-et lahat ha-cherev ha-mithappekhet lishmor et-derekh etz ha-chayyim*

| Hebrew | Transliteration | Literal | Note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| וַיְגָרֶשׁ אֶת־הָאָדָם | vaygaresh et-ha-adam | and he drove out the human | garash, to drive out, expel: Cain from the ground (4:14), Hagar (21:10), the nations from the land (Ex 23:28–31; 33:2). Stronger than "sent out" in 3:23; both verbs are used. With the article: the human, to the end of the chapter. |
| וַיַּשְׁכֵּן | vayyashken | and he stationed | shakhan (hiphil), to cause to dwell, set in place: the root of mishkan, the tabernacle (Ex 25:9), and of God's "dwelling" among Israel (Ex 25:8; 29:45–46). Its object here is the cherubim. Variant: LXX "and he settled him opposite the garden," making the man the object and adding "him"; MT and SP have the cherubim as object with no "him." Added to Judging variants. KJV: "placed." |
| מִקֶּדֶם לְגַן־עֵדֶן | miqqedem le-gan-Eden | from east of the garden of Eden | miqqedem, the word of 2:8, here with le-: "to the east of." This use fixes the spatial sense in 2:8. |
| הַכְּרֻבִים | ha-keruvim | the cherubim | keruv, plural keruvim, with the article as if known. The figures on the covenant chest (Ex 25:18–22), woven into the tabernacle curtains (Ex 26:1, 31) and carved in the temple (1 Kgs 6:23–35); God rides on one (2 Sam 22:11; Ps 18:11) and sits enthroned on them (1 Sam 4:4; Ps 80:2); Ezekiel sees them with four faces and wings (Ezek 10). About 91 uses; see Concordance and belief row 4. KJV: "Cherubims." |
| לַהַט הַחֶרֶב הַמִּתְהַפֶּכֶת | lahat ha-cherev ha-mithappekhet | the flame of the turning sword | lahat, flame (Ps 104:4, "flaming fire"). cherev, sword. hithpael participle of hafakh, turning itself over and over. Marked with et as a second object: the flame is stationed alongside the cherubim, not held by them. |
| לִשְׁמֹר | lishmor | to keep | shamar, the second verb of 2:15, where the human was to "keep" the garden. The cherubim now keep the way. In Num 3:7–8; 18:5–6 and 1 Chr 23:32 the same verb is the Levites' guard duty at the sanctuary, where cherubim are woven on the curtain (Ex 26:31). KJV: "to keep." |
| אֶת־דֶּרֶךְ עֵץ הַחַיִּים | et-derekh etz ha-chayyim | the way of the tree of life | derekh, road, way. The last words of the chapter name the tree of 2:9 and 3:22. |
