# Genesis 2 — verse log

One section per verse: bold translation line, pointed Hebrew, transliteration, then a table (Hebrew / Transliteration / Literal / Note). Notes state form and usage only. Rules in method/method.md.

## Genesis 2:1

**Translation: And the heavens and the earth were finished, and all their host.**

וַיְכֻלּוּ הַשָּׁמַיִם וְהָאָרֶץ וְכָל־צְבָאָם

*vaykhullu ha-shamayim ve-ha-aretz ve-khol-tzeva'am*

| Hebrew | Transliteration | Literal | Note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| וַיְכֻלּוּ | vaykhullu | and they were finished | Passive of *kalah*, to complete. The chapter division is medieval; the Hebrew runs straight on from 1:31. |
| וְכָל־צְבָאָם | ve-khol-tzeva'am | and all their host | *tzava* = an army, a host, an ordered company. Of the stars (Deut 4:19, Isa 40:26), of the beings around the throne (1 Kgs 22:19, "all the host of the heavens standing by him"), of Israel's armies (Ex 12:41). "Their" is plural: the host of heavens-and-earth together. The verse asserts that both realms have an ordered population at the end of the week. It does not say when or whether the heavenly part was made; ch. 1 narrated no such making. KJV: "host." |

## Genesis 2:2

**Translation: And God finished on the seventh day his work which he had made, and he ceased on the seventh day from all his work which he had made.**

וַיְכַל אֱלֹהִים בַּיּוֹם הַשְּׁבִיעִי מְלַאכְתּוֹ אֲשֶׁר עָשָׂה וַיִּשְׁבֹּת בַּיּוֹם הַשְּׁבִיעִי מִכָּל־מְלַאכְתּוֹ אֲשֶׁר עָשָׂה

*vaykhal Elohim ba-yom ha-shevi'i melakhto asher asah vayyishbot ba-yom ha-shevi'i mi-kol-melakhto asher asah*

| Hebrew | Transliteration | Literal | Note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| וַיְכַל אֱלֹהִים | vaykhal Elohim | and God finished | Active form of the same verb as 2:1. |
| בַּיּוֹם הַשְּׁבִיעִי | ba-yom ha-shevi'i | on the seventh day | **Textual variant.** MT: "seventh." LXX, Samaritan Pentateuch and Syriac: "sixth." Three witnesses against the MT, but all three remove the same difficulty (God finishing work on the day he ceases from it), and no scribe would create that difficulty from a smooth "sixth." The MT is the harder reading; the log follows it. Added to Judging variants. |
| מְלַאכְתּוֹ | melakhto | his work | *melakhah*: work, craft, occupation. The word of the Sabbath law (Ex 20:10, "any work"). |
| וַיִּשְׁבֹּת | vayyishbot | and he ceased | *shavat*: to cease, stop, leave off (Gen 8:22, seedtime and harvest "shall not cease"; Josh 5:12, the manna ceased). It is the root of *shabbat*. Not the word for repose: that is *nuach*, used of God in Ex 20:11, and in Ex 31:17 both appear together, "he ceased and was refreshed." KJV: "rested." |

## Genesis 2:3

**Translation: And God blessed the seventh day and made it holy, because on it he ceased from all his work which God created to make.**

וַיְבָרֶךְ אֱלֹהִים אֶת־יוֹם הַשְּׁבִיעִי וַיְקַדֵּשׁ אֹתוֹ כִּי בוֹ שָׁבַת מִכָּל־מְלַאכְתּוֹ אֲשֶׁר־בָּרָא אֱלֹהִים לַעֲשׂוֹת

*vayvarekh Elohim et-yom ha-shevi'i vayqaddesh oto ki vo shavat mi-kol-melakhto asher-bara Elohim la'asot*

| Hebrew | Transliteration | Literal | Note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| וַיְבָרֶךְ… אֶת־יוֹם | vayvarekh… et-yom | and he blessed… the day | Third blessing (fish and birds 1:22, humans 1:28, now a day). |
| וַיְקַדֵּשׁ אֹתוֹ | vayqaddesh oto | and he made it holy | *qadash*: to set apart, consecrate. **The first occurrence of "holy" in the Bible, and it is applied to a span of time**, not to a place, object or person. KJV: "sanctified." |
| אֲשֶׁר־בָּרָא אֱלֹהִים לַעֲשׂוֹת | asher-bara Elohim la'asot | which God created to make | *bara* and *asah* together, the chapter's two making-verbs joined: "created, in making" or "created so as to make." KJV "created and made" supplies an "and" the Hebrew lacks. |
| — | — | — | **No "evening and morning" and no day-count formula.** Every other day closed with it; the seventh does not. The text leaves the day open. |

## Genesis 2:4

**Translation: These are the begettings of the heavens and the earth in their being created, in the day of YHWH God's making earth and heavens.**

אֵלֶּה תוֹלְדוֹת הַשָּׁמַיִם וְהָאָרֶץ בְּהִבָּרְאָם בְּיוֹם עֲשׂוֹת יְהוָה אֱלֹהִים אֶרֶץ וְשָׁמָיִם

*elleh toledot ha-shamayim ve-ha-aretz be-hibbare'am be-yom asot YHWH Elohim eretz ve-shamayim*

| Hebrew | Transliteration | Literal | Note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| אֵלֶּה תוֹלְדוֹת | elleh toledot | these are the begettings | *toledot*, from *yalad*, to bear: what was begotten by, what came of. The heading formula of Genesis, used eleven times (5:1, 6:9, 10:1, 11:10, 11:27, 25:12, 25:19, 36:1, 36:9, 37:2); each time it introduces what came *from* the named thing, not the origin of the thing itself. So 2:4 heads what came of the heavens and earth. KJV: "generations." |
| בְּהִבָּרְאָם | be-hibbare'am | in their being created | Passive infinitive of *bara* with a suffix. The Masoretic scribes wrote the letter *he* in this word smaller than the rest (one of the Torah's marked letters); the consonants are unaffected. The main verse-divider (the *atnach*) sits under this word, so the Masoretic reading splits the verse here: 4a looks back, 4b looks forward. |
| בְּיוֹם עֲשׂוֹת | be-yom asot | in the day of making | *yom*, the same word as the six days, here for the whole making. The chapter's own usage shows the word covering more than a single day. |
| יְהוָה אֱלֹהִים | YHWH Elohim | YHWH God | **First appearance of the divine name.** The four consonants YHWH; the MT points them with the vowels of *Adonai* ("my Lord") as a standing instruction to say that word instead, so the vowels in the text are not the name's own. The pairing "YHWH Elohim" occurs 20 times in Gen 2–3 and almost nowhere else in the Torah. KJV: "the LORD God" (small capitals). |
| אֶרֶץ וְשָׁמָיִם | eretz ve-shamayim | earth and heavens | Reversed from 1:1, and without articles. This order occurs only here and in Ps 148:13. |
| — | — | — | **Variant:** LXX reads "This is the *book* of the begettings of heaven and earth," copying the wording of 5:1. Hebrew witnesses have no "book" here. |

## Genesis 2:5

**Translation: and no shrub of the field was yet in the earth, and no herb of the field had yet sprouted; for YHWH God had not caused rain upon the earth, and there was no human to work the ground;**

וְכֹל שִׂיחַ הַשָּׂדֶה טֶרֶם יִהְיֶה בָאָרֶץ וְכָל־עֵשֶׂב הַשָּׂדֶה טֶרֶם יִצְמָח כִּי לֹא הִמְטִיר יְהוָה אֱלֹהִים עַל־הָאָרֶץ וְאָדָם אַיִן לַעֲבֹד אֶת־הָאֲדָמָה

*ve-khol siach ha-sadeh terem yihyeh va-aretz ve-khol-esev ha-sadeh terem yitzmach ki lo himtir YHWH Elohim al-ha-aretz ve-adam ayin la'avod et-ha-adamah*

| Hebrew | Transliteration | Literal | Note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| שִׂיחַ הַשָּׂדֶה / עֵשֶׂב הַשָּׂדֶה | siach ha-sadeh / esev ha-sadeh | shrub of the field / herb of the field | Not the *deshe* of 1:11. "Of the field" marks open or cultivated country; the verse gives two conditions these plants lack: rain and a worker. |
| טֶרֶם | terem | not yet | Twice. The clause is background, as 1:2 was. |
| לֹא הִמְטִיר | lo himtir | had not caused rain | Causative of *matar*, rain. |
| וְאָדָם אַיִן | ve-adam ayin | and human: none | *adam* without article again; a collective or a kind, "no human." |
| לַעֲבֹד אֶת־הָאֲדָמָה | la'avod et-ha-adamah | to work the ground | *avad*: to work, serve, labor for; also the verb for serving a master and for worship (Ex 3:12). **First statement of human purpose in this account: to serve the ground.** *adam* and *adamah* sit side by side. KJV: "to till." |

## Genesis 2:6

**Translation: and a flow would go up from the earth and water all the face of the ground.**

וְאֵד יַעֲלֶה מִן־הָאָרֶץ וְהִשְׁקָה אֶת־כָּל־פְּנֵי־הָאֲדָמָה

*ve-ed ya'aleh min-ha-aretz ve-hishqah et-kol-penei-ha-adamah*

| Hebrew | Transliteration | Literal | Note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| וְאֵד | ve-ed | and a flow | Occurs only here and Job 36:27. Cognate with Akkadian *edû*, a surge of groundwater. LXX *pēgē* (a spring). "Mist" entered English through the Aramaic Targum. The Hebrew word is water coming *up* from the earth; the verse says so. |
| יַעֲלֶה | ya'aleh | would go up | Imperfect: repeated or habitual action. |
| וְהִשְׁקָה | ve-hishqah | and water | Causative of *shaqah*, to drink: give drink to. |

## Genesis 2:7

**Translation: And YHWH God formed the human, dust from the ground, and blew into his nostrils breath of life; and the human became a living soul.**

וַיִּיצֶר יְהוָה אֱלֹהִים אֶת־הָאָדָם עָפָר מִן־הָאֲדָמָה וַיִּפַּח בְּאַפָּיו נִשְׁמַת חַיִּים וַיְהִי הָאָדָם לְנֶפֶשׁ חַיָּה

*vayyitzer YHWH Elohim et-ha-adam afar min-ha-adamah vayyippach be-appav nishmat chayyim vayhi ha-adam le-nefesh chayyah*

| Hebrew | Transliteration | Literal | Note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| וַיִּיצֶר | vayyitzer | and he formed | *yatzar*: to shape, the potter's verb (Jer 18:2–6, Isa 29:16). A third making-verb after *bara* and *asah*. Spelled here with two *yod*s; in 2:19, of the animals, with one. The consonants differ; the meaning does not. |
| אֶת־הָאָדָם | et-ha-adam | the human | With article. Still "the human," not a name. |
| עָפָר מִן־הָאֲדָמָה | afar min-ha-adamah | dust from the ground | In apposition: formed the human \[as\] dust. *afar* is loose dry earth (the word of 3:19). |
| וַיִּפַּח בְּאַפָּיו | vayyippach be-appav | and he blew into his nostrils | *nafach*, to blow (on coals, Ezek 22:20; on the slain, Ezek 37:9). *appayim*, the two nostrils. |
| נִשְׁמַת חַיִּים | nishmat chayyim | breath of life | *neshamah*, breath; *chayyim* is a plural form used for the abstract "life." |
| וַיְהִי הָאָדָם לְנֶפֶשׁ חַיָּה | vayhi ha-adam le-nefesh chayyah | and the human became a living soul | *hayah l-* = became. **The phrase of 1:20, 24 and 30 (1:21 has it with the article).** The human becomes what the fish, birds and beasts already were. KJV: "a living soul" (here only); see belief row 9. |

## Genesis 2:8

**Translation: And YHWH God planted a garden in Eden, from east, and he put there the human whom he had formed.**

וַיִּטַּע יְהוָה אֱלֹהִים גַּן־בְּעֵדֶן מִקֶּדֶם וַיָּשֶׂם שָׁם אֶת־הָאָדָם אֲשֶׁר יָצָר

*vayyitta YHWH Elohim gan-be-Eden miqqedem vayyasem sham et-ha-adam asher yatzar*

| Hebrew | Transliteration | Literal | Note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| גַּן־בְּעֵדֶן | gan-be-Eden | a garden in Eden | **A garden *in* a place called Eden**, not "the Garden of Eden" as a name. The garden is inside a larger region; 2:10 has the river rising in Eden and flowing into the garden. As a common noun *eden* means luxury, delight (2 Sam 1:24, Ps 36:9). LXX: *paradeisos*, a Persian loanword for a walled park; KJV keeps "garden." |
| מִקֶּדֶם | miqqedem | from east | *qedem* is both "east" and "ancient time"; 3:24 ("east of the garden") fixes the spatial sense here. KJV: "eastward." |
| וַיָּשֶׂם שָׁם | vayyasem sham | and he put there | *sim*, to place. The human is made outside the garden (2:7) and brought in. |

## Genesis 2:9

**Translation: And YHWH God made sprout from the ground every tree desirable to the sight and good for food, and the tree of life in the midst of the garden, and the tree of the knowing of good and bad.**

וַיַּצְמַח יְהוָה אֱלֹהִים מִן־הָאֲדָמָה כָּל־עֵץ נֶחְמָד לְמַרְאֶה וְטוֹב לְמַאֲכָל וְעֵץ הַחַיִּים בְּתוֹךְ הַגָּן וְעֵץ הַדַּעַת טוֹב וָרָע

*vayyatzmach YHWH Elohim min-ha-adamah kol-etz nechmad le-mar'eh ve-tov le-ma'akhal ve-etz ha-chayyim betokh ha-gan ve-etz ha-da'at tov va-ra*

| Hebrew | Transliteration | Literal | Note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| נֶחְמָד לְמַרְאֶה | nechmad le-mar'eh | desirable to the sight | *chamad*: to desire, covet; the verb of the tenth commandment (Ex 20:17). The trees are described with the covet-word before any prohibition exists. |
| עֵץ הַחַיִּים | etz ha-chayyim | the tree of life | With article. "In the midst of the garden" attaches to it grammatically. |
| עֵץ הַדַּעַת טוֹב וָרָע | etz ha-da'at tov va-ra | the tree of the knowing of good and bad | *da'at* is "knowing" (a verbal noun). *ra* is "bad" in the widest sense: harmful, worthless, calamitous, evil. **The pair "knowing good and bad" is used elsewhere of a capacity**: children too young to have it (Deut 1:39), the very old who have lost it (2 Sam 19:35), the child Immanuel before he has it (Isa 7:15–16). In each it is practical discernment, the faculty of judging. KJV: "knowledge of good and evil." |

## Genesis 2:10

**Translation: And a river goes out from Eden to water the garden, and from there it divides and becomes four heads.**

וְנָהָר יֹצֵא מֵעֵדֶן לְהַשְׁקוֹת אֶת־הַגָּן וּמִשָּׁם יִפָּרֵד וְהָיָה לְאַרְבָּעָה רָאשִׁים

*ve-nahar yotze me-Eden le-hashqot et-ha-gan u-mi-sham yippared ve-hayah le-arba'ah rashim*

| Hebrew | Transliteration | Literal | Note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| יֹצֵא מֵעֵדֶן | yotze me-Eden | goes out from Eden | Participle, present tense: the river is described as still flowing when the verse is written. It rises in Eden and enters the garden; the garden is downstream of the source. |
| לְאַרְבָּעָה רָאשִׁים | le-arba'ah rashim | into four heads | *rosh*, head: here the branches or headwaters. |

## Genesis 2:11–12

**Translation: The name of the one is Pishon; it is the one circling all the land of the Havilah, where the gold is. And the gold of that land is good; there is the bdellium and the shoham stone.**

שֵׁם הָאֶחָד פִּישׁוֹן הוּא הַסֹּבֵב אֵת כָּל־אֶרֶץ הַחֲוִילָה אֲשֶׁר־שָׁם הַזָּהָב׃ וּזֲהַב הָאָרֶץ הַהִוא טוֹב שָׁם הַבְּדֹלַח וְאֶבֶן הַשֹּׁהַם

*shem ha-echad Pishon hu ha-sovev et kol-eretz ha-Chavilah asher-sham ha-zahav. u-zahav ha-aretz ha-hi tov sham ha-bedolach ve-even ha-shoham*

| Hebrew | Transliteration | Literal | Note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| פִּישׁוֹן | Pishon | Pishon | Named only here. Unidentified. |
| הַחֲוִילָה | ha-Chavilah | the Havilah | With article, as regions sometimes are. Elsewhere tied to Arabia (Gen 10:7, 29; 25:18; 1 Sam 15:7). |
| הַבְּדֹלַח | ha-bedolach | the bdellium | An aromatic resin, or a pearl; Num 11:7 compares manna to it. |
| אֶבֶן הַשֹּׁהַם | even ha-shoham | the shoham stone | Uncertain stone; the stone on the high priest's shoulder-pieces (Ex 28:9). KJV: "onyx." |

## Genesis 2:13

**Translation: And the name of the second river is Gihon; it is the one circling all the land of Cush.**

וְשֵׁם־הַנָּהָר הַשֵּׁנִי גִּיחוֹן הוּא הַסּוֹבֵב אֵת כָּל־אֶרֶץ כּוּשׁ

*ve-shem-ha-nahar ha-sheni Gichon hu ha-sovev et kol-eretz Kush*

| Hebrew | Transliteration | Literal | Note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| גִּיחוֹן | Gichon | Gihon | From *giach*, to burst forth. Also the name of Jerusalem's spring (1 Kgs 1:33). |
| כּוּשׁ | Kush | Cush | Usually the land south of Egypt (Nubia); but Gen 10:8 makes Cush the father of Nimrod, whose kingdom is in Mesopotamia. The Hebrew leaves which Cush open. **KJV decides: "Ethiopia."** |

## Genesis 2:14

**Translation: And the name of the third river is Hiddekel; it is the one going east of Asshur. And the fourth river, it is Perat.**

וְשֵׁם הַנָּהָר הַשְּׁלִישִׁי חִדֶּקֶל הוּא הַהֹלֵךְ קִדְמַת אַשּׁוּר וְהַנָּהָר הָרְבִיעִי הוּא פְרָת

*ve-shem ha-nahar ha-shelishi Chiddeqel hu ha-holekh qidmat Ashshur ve-ha-nahar ha-revi'i hu Ferat*

| Hebrew | Transliteration | Literal | Note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| חִדֶּקֶל | Chiddeqel | Hiddekel | The Tigris (Akkadian *Idiglat*). KJV keeps the Hebrew name here but uses the Greek name for the next river. |
| אַשּׁוּר | Ashshur | Asshur | Assyria, the city or the land. |
| פְרָת | Perat | Perat | The Euphrates (Akkadian *Purattu*). KJV: "Euphrates." **Two of the four rivers are real and locatable, in Mesopotamia; two are not.** The text places the garden on a real map and describes it in the present tense. |

## Genesis 2:15

**Translation: And YHWH God took the human and settled him in the garden of Eden to work it and to keep it.**

וַיִּקַּח יְהוָה אֱלֹהִים אֶת־הָאָדָם וַיַּנִּחֵהוּ בְגַן־עֵדֶן לְעָבְדָהּ וּלְשָׁמְרָהּ

*vayyiqqach YHWH Elohim et-ha-adam vayyannichehu ve-gan-Eden le-ovdah u-le-shomrah*

| Hebrew | Transliteration | Literal | Note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| וַיַּנִּחֵהוּ | vayyannichehu | and he settled him | Causative of *nuach*, the rest-verb of Ex 20:11: "caused him to rest, set him down." Not the plain "put" of 2:8. KJV: "put." |
| לְעָבְדָהּ וּלְשָׁמְרָהּ | le-ovdah u-le-shomrah | to work it and to keep it | *avad* (work, serve) and *shamar* (keep, guard, watch). **This pair of verbs is the pair used for the Levites' duty in the sanctuary** (Num 3:7–8, 8:26, 18:5–6: "to serve and to keep"). *shamar* is also the cherubim's task in 3:24. The suffixes are feminine though *gan* is masculine; they may refer to *adamah*, the ground. KJV: "to dress it and to keep it." |

## Genesis 2:16

**Translation: And YHWH God commanded the human, saying, "From every tree of the garden, eating you may eat;**

וַיְצַו יְהוָה אֱלֹהִים עַל־הָאָדָם לֵאמֹר מִכֹּל עֵץ־הַגָּן אָכֹל תֹּאכֵל

*vaytzav YHWH Elohim al-ha-adam lemor mi-kol etz-ha-gan akhol tokhel*

| Hebrew | Transliteration | Literal | Note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| וַיְצַו | vaytzav | and he commanded | *tzavah*, the root of *mitzvah*. **The first command.** Addressed to the human alone; the woman is not yet formed. |
| אָכֹל תֹּאכֵל | akhol tokhel | eating you may eat | Infinitive + finite verb, Hebrew's way of stressing a verb: "you may freely eat." Singular "you." |

## Genesis 2:17

**Translation: but from the tree of the knowing of good and bad you shall not eat from it; for in the day of your eating from it, dying you shall die."**

וּמֵעֵץ הַדַּעַת טוֹב וָרָע לֹא תֹאכַל מִמֶּנּוּ כִּי בְּיוֹם אֲכָלְךָ מִמֶּנּוּ מוֹת תָּמוּת

*u-me-etz ha-da'at tov va-ra lo tokhal mimmennu ki be-yom akholkha mimmennu mot tamut*

| Hebrew | Transliteration | Literal | Note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| לֹא תֹאכַל | lo tokhal | you shall not eat | Singular. The prohibition in the text is given to one person. |
| בְּיוֹם אֲכָלְךָ | be-yom akholkha | in the day of your eating | *yom* again, as in 2:4. |
| מוֹת תָּמוּת | mot tamut | dying you shall die | The same stressed form as v.16. **This is the fixed formula of a death sentence** in Hebrew law and narrative (Gen 20:7; Ex 19:12; 1 Sam 14:44; 1 Kgs 2:37, 42). In 1 Kgs 2:37 Solomon tells Shimei "on the day you go out… dying you shall die"; Shimei goes out, and is executed some time later. In the Bible's own usage the formula pronounces the sentence; it does not time the execution. KJV: "thou shalt surely die." |

## Genesis 2:18

**Translation: And YHWH God said, "Not good, the human's being alone; I will make for him a helper as his counterpart."**

וַיֹּאמֶר יְהוָה אֱלֹהִים לֹא־טוֹב הֱיוֹת הָאָדָם לְבַדּוֹ אֶעֱשֶׂהּ־לּוֹ עֵזֶר כְּנֶגְדּוֹ

*vayyomer YHWH Elohim lo-tov heyot ha-adam levaddo e'eseh-lo ezer kenegdo*

| Hebrew | Transliteration | Literal | Note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| לֹא־טוֹב | lo-tov | not good | The first "not good" after seven "good"s. Said by God before the human has done anything. |
| אֶעֱשֶׂהּ־לּוֹ | e'eseh-lo | I will make for him | **Singular, first person.** The LXX reads "let *us* make," harmonizing with 1:26. The MT singular is the harder reading; added to Judging variants. |
| עֵזֶר | ezer | a helper | Help, one who helps. Of its 21 uses, 16 are of God helping Israel (Ex 18:4; Deut 33:7, 26, 29; Ps 33:20, 121:1–2). The word carries no rank; the helper is usually the stronger party. |
| כְּנֶגְדּוֹ | kenegdo | as his counterpart | *ke-* (like) + *neged* (in front of, opposite, matching) + *-o* (him): "like what stands opposite him," a match, a counterpart. Only here and v.20. KJV: "meet for him," where "meet" is 1611 English for fitting, suitable. |

## Genesis 2:19

**Translation: And YHWH God formed from the ground every living-thing of the field and every flyer of the heavens, and brought \[them\] to the human to see what he would call it; and whatever the human called it, living soul, that was its name.**

וַיִּצֶר יְהוָה אֱלֹהִים מִן־הָאֲדָמָה כָּל־חַיַּת הַשָּׂדֶה וְאֵת כָּל־עוֹף הַשָּׁמַיִם וַיָּבֵא אֶל־הָאָדָם לִרְאוֹת מַה־יִּקְרָא־לוֹ וְכֹל אֲשֶׁר יִקְרָא־לוֹ הָאָדָם נֶפֶשׁ חַיָּה הוּא שְׁמוֹ

*vayyitzer YHWH Elohim min-ha-adamah kol-chayyat ha-sadeh ve-et kol-of ha-shamayim vayyave el-ha-adam lir'ot mah-yiqra-lo ve-khol asher yiqra-lo ha-adam nefesh chayyah hu shemo*

| Hebrew | Transliteration | Literal | Note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| וַיִּצֶר | vayyitzer | and he formed | One *yod* (two in 2:7). The ordinary sequential narrative form, "formed." English versions that print "had formed" are choosing a pluperfect the Hebrew does not mark. KJV: "formed." |
| מִן־הָאֲדָמָה | min-ha-adamah | from the ground | The animals and birds are made of the same material as the human (2:7). |
| מַה־יִּקְרָא־לוֹ | mah-yiqra-lo | what he would call it | *qara l-*, the naming construction God used in 1:5, 8, 10. The human now does what God did. |
| נֶפֶשׁ חַיָּה | nefesh chayyah | living soul | Fifth use, of the animals being named. KJV: "every living creature." |

## Genesis 2:20

**Translation: And the human called names to every beast and to the flyer of the heavens and to every living-thing of the field; but for human he did not find a helper as his counterpart.**

וַיִּקְרָא הָאָדָם שֵׁמוֹת לְכָל־הַבְּהֵמָה וּלְעוֹף הַשָּׁמַיִם וּלְכֹל חַיַּת הַשָּׂדֶה וּלְאָדָם לֹא־מָצָא עֵזֶר כְּנֶגְדּוֹ

*vayyiqra ha-adam shemot le-khol-ha-behemah u-le-of ha-shamayim u-le-khol chayyat ha-sadeh u-le-adam lo-matza ezer kenegdo*

| Hebrew | Transliteration | Literal | Note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| וּלְאָדָם | u-le-adam | but for human | **Vowel-dependent.** The MT points this without the article (*le-adam*, not *la-adam*), which makes it read like a name; the consonants לאדם are identical either way. Everywhere else in ch. 2–3 the word has the article, "the human." **This is the first place KJV prints "Adam"** as a name. Whether it is a name here rests on a medieval vowel. |
| לֹא־מָצָא | lo-matza | he did not find | The subject is not stated; it is the human or God. |

## Genesis 2:21

**Translation: And YHWH God caused a deep sleep to fall upon the human, and he slept; and he took one of his sides, and closed flesh in its place.**

וַיַּפֵּל יְהוָה אֱלֹהִים תַּרְדֵּמָה עַל־הָאָדָם וַיִּישָׁן וַיִּקַּח אַחַת מִצַּלְעֹתָיו וַיִּסְגֹּר בָּשָׂר תַּחְתֶּנָּה

*vayyappel YHWH Elohim tardemah al-ha-adam vayyishan vayyiqqach achat mi-tzal'otav vayyisgor basar tachtennah*

| Hebrew | Transliteration | Literal | Note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| תַּרְדֵּמָה | tardemah | a deep sleep | A God-sent, overpowering sleep (Gen 15:12, Abram; 1 Sam 26:12; Isa 29:10). |
| אַחַת מִצַּלְעֹתָיו | achat mi-tzal'otav | one of his sides | ***tzela* means "side" everywhere else in the Bible**: the side of the covenant chest (Ex 25:12), the side of the tabernacle (Ex 26:20), the side-chambers of the temple (1 Kgs 6:5), the side of a hill (2 Sam 16:13). It is never "rib" elsewhere. "Rib" comes from the Greek *pleura* (which means both) and from later tradition. KJV: "ribs." |
| וַיִּסְגֹּר בָּשָׂר תַּחְתֶּנָּה | vayyisgor basar tachtennah | and closed flesh in its place | *tachat*, under, in place of. |

## Genesis 2:22

**Translation: And YHWH God built the side which he had taken from the human into a woman, and brought her to the human.**

וַיִּבֶן יְהוָה אֱלֹהִים אֶת־הַצֵּלָע אֲשֶׁר־לָקַח מִן־הָאָדָם לְאִשָּׁה וַיְבִאֶהָ אֶל־הָאָדָם

*vayyiven YHWH Elohim et-ha-tzela asher-laqach min-ha-adam le-ishah vayvi'eha el-ha-adam*

| Hebrew | Transliteration | Literal | Note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| וַיִּבֶן | vayyiven | and he built | *banah*, to build: the fourth making-verb (*bara*, *asah*, *yatzar*, *banah*). The verb for houses, cities, altars. |
| לְאִשָּׁה | le-ishah | into a woman | *ishah*: woman. First appearance. Hebrew has no separate word for "wife"; *ishah* is both. KJV: "a woman" here, "wife" in v.24–25. |

## Genesis 2:23

**Translation: And the human said, "This time: bone from my bones and flesh from my flesh. To this shall be called woman, for from man this was taken."**

וַיֹּאמֶר הָאָדָם זֹאת הַפַּעַם עֶצֶם מֵעֲצָמַי וּבָשָׂר מִבְּשָׂרִי לְזֹאת יִקָּרֵא אִשָּׁה כִּי מֵאִישׁ לֻקֳחָה־זֹּאת

*vayyomer ha-adam zot ha-pa'am etzem me-atzamai u-vasar mi-besari le-zot yiqqare ishah ki me-ish luqochah-zot*

| Hebrew | Transliteration | Literal | Note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| זֹאת הַפַּעַם | zot ha-pa'am | this, the time | "This time," "at last." **The human's first speech**, and it is verse. |
| עֶצֶם מֵעֲצָמַי וּבָשָׂר מִבְּשָׂרִי | etzem me-atzamai u-vasar mi-besari | bone from my bones and flesh from my flesh | **A kinship formula**, not an anatomical report: Laban says it of Jacob (29:14), Abimelech's kin of him (Judg 9:2), the tribes of David (2 Sam 5:1). It means "my own kind, my family." |
| יִקָּרֵא אִשָּׁה | yiqqare ishah | shall be called woman | Passive, and a category word, not a name. The human named the animals (*qara l-* with a name); here he does not name her. Her name comes in 3:20. |
| אִשָּׁה / אִישׁ | ishah / ish | woman / man | ***ish*, man, appears here for the first time**, in the same breath as *ishah*. Until now the male has only been *adam*, the human; he becomes *ish* when *ishah* exists. The two words are paired by sound; English "woman/man" happens to echo it. |

## Genesis 2:24

**Translation: Therefore a man leaves his father and his mother and clings to his woman, and they become one flesh.**

עַל־כֵּן יַעֲזָב־אִישׁ אֶת־אָבִיו וְאֶת־אִמּוֹ וְדָבַק בְּאִשְׁתּוֹ וְהָיוּ לְבָשָׂר אֶחָד

*al-ken ya'azov-ish et-aviv ve-et-immo ve-davaq be-ishto ve-hayu le-vasar echad*

| Hebrew | Transliteration | Literal | Note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| עַל־כֵּן | al-ken | therefore | **The narrator's voice**, not the human's: the verbs are habitual ("a man leaves"), and the human has no father or mother. A comment on what the story explains. |
| יַעֲזָב־אִישׁ | ya'azov-ish | a man leaves | *azav*: leave, forsake (Deut 31:16, of forsaking God). It is the man who leaves. |
| וְדָבַק | ve-davaq | and clings | *davaq*: to stick, cling (Ruth 1:14; Deut 10:20, cling to YHWH). KJV: "cleave." |
| בְּאִשְׁתּוֹ | be-ishto | to his woman | Same word as v.22–23. KJV: "wife." |
| וְהָיוּ לְבָשָׂר אֶחָד | ve-hayu le-vasar echad | and they become one flesh | *basar*, flesh, is also "kin" (v.23). **Variant:** LXX, Samaritan, Syriac and Vulgate read "and *the two* shall become one flesh"; so do the Gospel quotations. The MT has "they" with no "two." Added to Judging variants. KJV follows the MT here ("they shall be") though it prints "they twain" in Matt 19:5. |

## Genesis 2:25

**Translation: And the two of them were naked, the human and his woman, and they were not ashamed \[before each other\].**

וַיִּהְיוּ שְׁנֵיהֶם עֲרוּמִּים הָאָדָם וְאִשְׁתּוֹ וְלֹא יִתְבֹּשָׁשׁוּ

*vayyihyu sheneihem arummim ha-adam ve-ishto ve-lo yitboshashu*

| Hebrew | Transliteration | Literal | Note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| עֲרוּמִּים | arummim | naked | *arom*, naked. **The next word of the story (3:1) is *arum*, "shrewd," spelled with the same consonants.** The chapter break, added in the Middle Ages, separates a deliberate pair. |
| וְלֹא יִתְבֹּשָׁשׁוּ | ve-lo yitboshashu | and they were not ashamed \[before each other\] | Reciprocal form of *bosh*, to be ashamed: shame toward one another. |
