# Genesis — readable translation

The running translation, with English grammar allowed to touch it and nothing else. Where a verse was rephrased, the literal line stands beneath it between ⸤ ⸥. "It was" in every "God saw that it was good" is supplied; Hebrew drops the verb, and it is not marked each time. Where a widely read rendering differs from the text in a way that has shaped what people take the verse to mean, a line in italics says what the text has and why. No version is named; the full account for each is in belief/translation-to-belief.md.

## Chapter 1

**1** In beginning, God created the heavens and the earth.

*"Heavens," not "heaven": the word is plural, and Hebrew has no singular for it; Deut 10:14 says "the heavens and the heavens of heavens." "In beginning": there is no "the."*

**2** And the earth was waste and empty, and darkness was upon the face of the deep, and the spirit of God was hovering upon the face of the waters.

⸤ *and darkness upon the face of the deep, and the spirit of God hovering* ⸥

**3** And God said, "Let light be." And light was.

**4** And God saw the light, that it was good. And God separated between the light and between the darkness.

**5** And God called the light "Day," and the darkness he called "Night." And there was evening and there was morning: day one.

*"Day one," not "the first day": a number, not an ordinal, and no "the." Compare "a second day" (1:8) and "the sixth day" (1:31), where the article first appears.*

**6** And God said, "Let an expanse be in the midst of the waters, and let it be a separator between waters and waters."

*"Expanse": a beaten-out sheet; the root means to hammer thin. The old word "firmament" kept that sense.*

**7** And God made the expanse, and separated between the waters which are from beneath the expanse and between the waters which are from above the expanse. And it was so.

**8** And God called the expanse "Heavens." And there was evening and there was morning: a second day.

**9** And God said, "Let the waters from under the heavens be gathered to one place, and let the dry ground be seen." And it was so.

⸤ *the dry \[ground\]* ⸥

**10** And God called the dry ground "Earth," and the gathering of the waters he called "Seas." And God saw that it was good.

⸤ *the dry \[ground\]* ⸥

*"Earth" is the dry ground the text has just named. It is the same word as "land" in "the land of Egypt" (Gen 13:10) and "the land of Canaan" (Gen 12:5). It is not the planet.*

**11** And God said, "Let the earth sprout sprouts: herb seeding seed, fruit tree making fruit to its kind, whose seed is in it, upon the earth." And it was so.

*"To its kind": an everyday word for sort. Lev 11:14–19 uses it to group hawks, ravens and bats as "kinds" of flyers. It says plants carry their own seed; it names no fixed biological unit.*

**12** And the earth brought out sprouts: herb seeding seed to its kind, and tree making fruit whose seed is in it, to its kind. And God saw that it was good.

**13** And there was evening and there was morning: a third day.

**14** And God said, "Let lights be in the expanse of the heavens, to separate between the day and between the night; and let them be for signs and for appointed times and for days and years.

*"Appointed times," not "seasons": the word for the festival calendar, "these are the appointed times of YHWH" (Lev 23:2, 4), and in Ps 104:19, "he made the moon for appointed times." Weather seasons have other words (Gen 8:22).*

**15** And let them be for lights in the expanse of the heavens to give light upon the earth." And it was so.

**16** And God made the two great lights: the great light for the rule of the day, and the small light for the rule of the night, and the stars.

**17** And God set them in the expanse of the heavens to give light upon the earth,

**18** and to rule in the day and in the night, and to separate between the light and between the darkness. And God saw that it was good.

**19** And there was evening and there was morning: a fourth day.

**20** And God said, "Let the waters swarm swarmers, living soul; and let flyer fly upon the earth, upon the face of the expanse of the heavens."

*"Living soul": the same two words used of the land animals (1:24) and of the human (2:7). The birds are a separate command; the waters do not produce them.*

**21** And God created the great tanninim, and every living soul that moves, with which the waters swarmed, to their kinds, and every winged flyer to its kind. And God saw that it was good.

*"Tanninim," not "whales": the same word is the serpent Aaron's rod becomes (Ex 7:9–12) and the river-beast that stands for Pharaoh (Ezek 29:3). Ps 148:7 calls on tanninim to praise with the rest of creation. No English word covers it, so the Hebrew is kept.*

**22** And God blessed them, saying, "Be fruitful and multiply and fill the waters in the seas; and let the flyer multiply in the earth."

**23** And there was evening and there was morning: a fifth day.

**24** And God said, "Let the earth bring out living soul to its kind: beast and creeper and living-thing of earth to its kind." And it was so.

**25** And God made the living-thing of the earth to its kind, and the beast to its kind, and every creeper of the ground to its kind. And God saw that it was good.

**26** And God said, "Let us make human in our image, as our likeness; and let them rule over the fish of the sea and over the flyer of the heavens and over the beast and over all the earth and over every creeper that creeps upon the earth."

*"Human," not "man" or "Adam": a collective, male and female, with a plural "them"; Gen 5:2 says God "named them human." "Image" is the ordinary word for a statue (Num 33:52; 2 Kgs 11:18). "Rule" is a king's word (Ps 72:8).*

**27** And God created the human in his image; in the image of God he created him; male and female he created them.

**28** And God blessed them, and God said to them, "Be fruitful and multiply and fill the earth and subdue it; and rule over the fish of the sea and over the flyer of the heavens and over every living-thing that moves upon the earth."

**29** And God said, "Behold, I have given to you every herb seeding seed which is upon the face of all the earth, and every tree in which is fruit of tree seeding seed; to you it shall be for food.

**30** And to every living-thing of the earth and to every flyer of the heavens and to every creeper upon the earth in which is living soul, every green of herb for food." And it was so.

**31** And God saw all that he had made, and behold, it was very good. And there was evening and there was morning: the sixth day.

⸤ *and behold, very good* ⸥

## Chapter 2

**1** And the heavens and the earth were finished, and all their host.

**2** And God finished on the seventh day his work which he had made, and he ceased on the seventh day from all his work which he had made.

*"Ceased," not "rested": the verb means to stop, as in "seedtime and harvest shall not cease" (Gen 8:22), and is the root of "Sabbath." Rest is a different verb; Ex 31:17 uses both: "he ceased and was refreshed."*

**3** And God blessed the seventh day and made it holy, because on it he ceased from all his work which God created to make.

**4** These are the begettings of the heavens and the earth in their being created, in the day of YHWH God's making earth and heavens,

*"YHWH": a name, four letters, first used here; "this is my name forever" (Ex 3:15). "The LORD" is the word spoken in its place, not a translation.*

**5** and no shrub of the field was yet in the earth, and no herb of the field had yet sprouted; for YHWH God had not caused rain upon the earth, and there was no human to work the ground;

**6** and a flow would go up from the earth and water all the face of the ground.

*"A flow," not "a mist": water rising from the earth. The word's only other use is Job 36:27.*

**7** And YHWH God formed the human, dust from the ground, and blew into his nostrils breath of life; and the human became a living soul.

*"A living soul": what the fish, birds and beasts were called in 1:20, 21, 24 and 30. The human becomes what they already were.*

**8** And YHWH God planted a garden in Eden, from east, and he put there the human whom he had formed.

*A garden inside a region called Eden, on the ground, fed by a river that rises in Eden and runs into the garden (2:10), with the Tigris and Euphrates among its branches (2:14). "Paradise" is a Greek word from a later book.*

**9** And YHWH God made sprout from the ground every tree desirable to the sight and good for food, and the tree of life in the midst of the garden, and the tree of the knowing of good and bad.

*"Good and bad," not "good and evil": "bad" in the widest sense. Elsewhere the phrase is the judgment small children do not yet have (Deut 1:39), the old lose (2 Sam 19:35), and a child grows into (Isa 7:15–16).*

**10** And a river goes out from Eden to water the garden, and from there it divides and becomes four heads.

**11** The name of the one is Pishon; it is the one circling all the land of the Havilah, where the gold is.

**12** And the gold of that land is good; there is the bdellium and the shoham stone.

**13** And the name of the second river is Gihon; it is the one circling all the land of Cush.

**14** And the name of the third river is Hiddekel; it is the one going east of Asshur. And the fourth river, it is Perat.

**15** And YHWH God took the human and settled him in the garden of Eden to work it and to keep it.

**16** And YHWH God commanded the human, saying, "From every tree of the garden, eating you may eat;

**17** but from the tree of the knowing of good and bad you shall not eat from it; for in the day of your eating from it, dying you shall die."

*"Dying you shall die": the fixed wording of a death sentence (Gen 20:7; Ex 19:12). In 1 Kgs 2:37 Solomon says it to Shimei with the same "on the day," and Shimei is executed years later (2:42–46). It pronounces the penalty, not the day it is carried out.*

**18** And YHWH God said, "It is not good for the human to be alone; I will make for him a helper as his counterpart."

⸤ *Not good, the human's being alone* ⸥

*"A helper as his counterpart," not "help meet": a match. The word for helper is most often used of God helping Israel: "my help comes from YHWH" (Ps 121:2; also Ex 18:4; Deut 33:29).*

**19** And YHWH God formed from the ground every living-thing of the field and every flyer of the heavens, and brought them to the human to see what he would call it; and whatever the human called it, living soul, that was its name.

⸤ *brought \[them\]* ⸥

**20** And the human called names to every beast and to the flyer of the heavens and to every living-thing of the field; but for human he did not find a helper as his counterpart.

*"Human," not "Adam": the letters are the same as in 2:7 and 2:19, "the human." The name rests on a vowel mark added a thousand years later.*

**21** And YHWH God caused a deep sleep to fall upon the human, and he slept; and he took one of his sides, and closed flesh in its place.

*"Side," not "rib": the word means side in every other place it is used: the side of the tabernacle (Ex 26:20), the side of the covenant chest where the rings go (Ex 25:12), the side of a hill (2 Sam 16:13), the side-chambers of the temple (1 Kgs 6:5). "Rib" came through Greek, where one word means both.*

**22** And YHWH God built the side which he had taken from the human into a woman, and brought her to the human.

**23** And the human said, "This time: bone from my bones and flesh from my flesh. To this shall be called woman, for from man this was taken."

**24** Therefore a man leaves his father and his mother and clings to his woman, and they become one flesh.

*"They," not "the two": "two" is in the Greek translation and in the Gospels (Matt 19:5; Mark 10:8), not in the Hebrew here.*

**25** And the two of them were naked, the human and his woman, and they were not ashamed before each other.

⸤ *not ashamed \[before each other\]* ⸥

## Chapter 3

**1** And the serpent was shrewd, more than every living-thing of the field which YHWH God had made; and he said to the woman, "Is it even so, that God said, 'You shall not eat from every tree of the garden'?"

⸤ *Even so, that God said* ⸥

*"Shrewd": the word Proverbs uses for the prudent (Prov 14:8, 15; 22:3). Its letters are the letters of "naked" in 2:25; the two verses sit side by side. "Serpent" is the everyday snake word (Num 21:6–9; Eccl 10:8), and the verse says it was one of the living-things God made.*

**2** And the woman said to the serpent, "From the fruit of the tree of the garden we may eat;

**3** but from the fruit of the tree which is in the midst of the garden, God said, 'You shall not eat from it, and you shall not touch it, lest you die.'"

**4** And the serpent said to the woman, "Not dying you shall die;

**5** for God knows that in the day you eat from it, your eyes will be opened and you will be like God, knowing good and bad."

⸤ *in the day of your eating from it… knowers of good and bad* ⸥

*"Like God": the same word is "gods" in some readings; the Hebrew form is one word for both, and the verse does not settle it. In 3:22 the narrator's phrase is "like one of us."*

**6** And the woman saw that the tree was good for food, and that it was a desire to the eyes, and that the tree was desirable to make wise; and she took from its fruit and ate; and she gave also to her man with her, and he ate.

⸤ *and the tree desirable to make wise* ⸥

*"Fruit": no species is named anywhere in the chapter.*

**7** And the eyes of the two of them were opened, and they knew that they were naked; and they sewed fig leaf and made themselves belts.

⸤ *made for themselves belts* ⸥

**8** And they heard the sound of YHWH God walking about in the garden at the wind of the day; and the human and his woman hid themselves from the face of YHWH God in the midst of the tree of the garden.

*"At the wind of the day": the ordinary word for wind, breath or spirit, the word of 1:2. "The cool of the day" is a paraphrase.*

**9** And YHWH God called to the human and said to him, "Where are you?"

**10** And he said, "I heard your sound in the garden, and I was afraid, because I am naked; and I hid myself."

⸤ *Your sound I heard in the garden* ⸥

**11** And he said, "Who told you that you are naked? Have you eaten from the tree which I commanded you not to eat from?"

⸤ *From the tree which I commanded you not to eat from it, have you eaten?* ⸥

**12** And the human said, "The woman whom you gave to be with me, she gave to me from the tree, and I ate."

⸤ *whom you gave with me* ⸥

**13** And YHWH God said to the woman, "What is this you have done?" And the woman said, "The serpent deceived me, and I ate."

**14** And YHWH God said to the serpent, "Because you have done this, cursed are you from every beast and from every living-thing of the field; upon your belly you shall go, and dust you shall eat all the days of your life.

**15** And I will set enmity between you and between the woman, and between your seed and between her seed; he shall strike you on the head, and you shall strike him on the heel."

⸤ *And enmity I will set… he shall strike you \[on the\] head, and you shall strike him \[on the\] heel* ⸥

*One verb, "strike," is used of both blows; renderings that give the two sides two different verbs add a difference the Hebrew does not have. "He" is the pronoun for "seed," a collective word used of plants (1:11) and of offspring, one or many (15:5); the Hebrew letters can also be read "she," and one old translation did. "Seed," "head" and "heel" are the ordinary words.*

**16** To the woman he said, "Multiplying I will multiply your toil and your pregnancy; in toil you shall bear sons; and your desire shall be to your man, and he shall rule over you."

⸤ *and to your man your desire* ⸥

*"Toil": the same word is spoken to the man in the next verse, "in toil you shall eat." It is the word for hard labor, not for grief (Gen 5:29; Prov 14:23). "Desire" rests on one Hebrew letter; two old translations read "return," and both Hebrew lines read "desire." "He shall rule" is a statement of what will be, in the same form as "you shall bear" and "it shall sprout" (3:18); the verb is the one used of the lamps ruling day and night (1:18).*

**17** And to the human he said, "Because you listened to the voice of your woman and ate from the tree of which I commanded you, 'You shall not eat from it,' cursed is the ground on account of you; in toil you shall eat from it all the days of your life.

⸤ *And to human he said… which I commanded you, saying… in toil you shall eat it* ⸥

*"The human," not "Adam": the letters are those of 3:8, 9, 12, 20, 22 and 24, "the human." Here and in 3:21 the name rests on a vowel mark, as in 2:20. The curse falls on the ground; no curse is spoken on the man or the woman in this chapter.*

**18** And thorn and thistle it shall sprout for you, and you shall eat the herb of the field.

**19** In the sweat of your nostrils you shall eat bread, until you return to the ground, for from it you were taken; for dust you are, and to dust you shall return."

⸤ *until your returning to the ground* ⸥

**20** And the human called the name of his woman Chavvah, for she was mother of all living.

*"Chavvah": the name is explained in the verse itself by the word "living"; the Greek translation rendered it "Life." "Eve" is the Latin form of the name and shows no link to the explanation beside it.*

**21** And YHWH God made for the human and for his woman tunics of skin, and clothed them.

⸤ *made for human and for his woman* ⸥

**22** And YHWH God said, "Behold, the human has become like one of us, to know good and bad; and now, lest he send his hand and take also from the tree of life and eat and live forever…"

*"Us": the plural of 1:26, "let us make," and 11:7, "let us go down." The verse says the human has become like one of a group and does not say who the group is.*

**23** And YHWH God sent him out from the garden of Eden to work the ground from which he was taken.

**24** And he drove out the human, and he stationed to the east of the garden of Eden the cherubim and the flame of the turning sword, to keep the way of the tree of life.

⸤ *and he stationed from east of the garden of Eden* ⸥

*"Keep": the verb the human was given in 2:15, "to work it and to keep it." The cherubim are the figures on the covenant chest and the tabernacle curtains (Ex 25:18–20; 26:1, 31). "East" is the direction of 2:8, so the garden has a side and the human is outside it, on the ground he was taken from (3:19, 23).*

## Chapter 4

**1** And the human knew Chavvah his woman, and she conceived and bore Qayin; and she said, "I have acquired a man with YHWH."

*"The human," not "Adam": the letters carry the article here, as in 3:8–24; the name without the article first appears in the letters at 4:25. "With YHWH": the word is "with," as in "walked with God" (5:22, 24); "from" is a different word, not used here.*

**2** And she again bore his brother, Hevel. And Hevel was a shepherd of flock, and Qayin was a worker of ground.

**3** And it was, at the end of days, and Qayin brought from the fruit of the ground an offering to YHWH.

*"Offering": the word for a gift carried to a superior (Gen 32:14; 43:11; Judg 3:15) and for the grain offering of the law (Lev 2:1). The chapter has no altar and no word for sacrifice; both brothers bring the same thing, named by the same word.*

**4** And Hevel also brought from the firstborn of his flock and from their fat; and YHWH looked to Hevel and to his offering;

⸤ *And Hevel brought, also he* ⸥

*"Looked to": the verb means to look toward, to turn one's gaze (Ex 5:9; Isa 17:7; 31:1). The text says he looked at one and not at the other (4:5) and states no reason in either verse.*

**5** but to Qayin and to his offering he did not look. And it burned to Qayin greatly, and his face fell.

**6** And YHWH said to Qayin, "Why has it burned to you, and why has your face fallen?

**7** Is it not: if you do well, there is a lifting; and if you do not do well, sin is lying down at the door; and its desire is to you, and you shall rule over it."

⸤ *Is it not: if you do well, a lifting; and if you do not do well, at the door sin \[is\] lying down; and to you its desire* ⸥

*"A lifting": the noun of the verb in "his face fell" reversed (4:5–6) and of "more than to bear" (4:13); the verb lifts a face (Job 11:15), carries a load (Gen 45:23) and lifts away guilt (Ex 34:7). "Lying down" is the verb for flocks at rest (Gen 29:2; Ex 23:5) and for a curse settling on a man (Deut 29:19). "Sin" is the word's first use. "Desire" and "rule over" are the two words said to the woman in 3:16, in the same order.*

**8** And Qayin said to Hevel his brother. And it was, when they were in the field, and Qayin rose up against Hevel his brother and killed him.

⸤ *in their being in the field* ⸥

*The Hebrew gives no words after "said." Four old witnesses, one of them Hebrew, add "Let us go to the field." The shorter text is kept here; the case is set out in the variant table.*

**9** And YHWH said to Qayin, "Where is Hevel your brother?" And he said, "I do not know. Am I my brother's keeper?"

⸤ *A keeper of my brother \[am\] I?* ⸥

*"Keeper": the verb of 2:15, "to work it and to keep it," and of the cherubim in 3:24, "to keep the way." The same word is a watchman (Ps 121:4; 127:1).*

**10** And he said, "What have you done? The voice of the bloods of your brother are crying to me from the ground.

*"Bloods": plural, the form Hebrew uses for shed blood (Gen 37:26; 2 Sam 16:8); "crying" is plural with it. The ground is the one the human was taken from (2:7; 3:19).*

**11** And now, cursed are you from the ground which opened its mouth to take the bloods of your brother from your hand.

*"Cursed are you from": the wording of 3:14 to the serpent. This is the third curse (3:14, 17) and the first spoken on a human; "from" can mark a comparison or a separation, and the verse allows either.*

**12** When you work the ground, it shall not again give its strength to you; staggering and wandering you shall be in the earth."

**13** And Qayin said to YHWH, "My iniquity is too great to bear."

⸤ *Great is my iniquity, more than to bear* ⸥

*"Iniquity": the word for guilt and for what guilt brings (Gen 19:15; 1 Sam 28:10); "bear" is the "lifting" of 4:7, the verb for carrying a load and for forgiving (Ex 34:7; Num 14:18). "Punishment" is one sense the word can carry, not a separate word in the verse.*

**14** Behold, you have driven me out today from upon the face of the ground, and from your face I shall be hidden; and I shall be staggering and wandering in the earth, and it shall be, everyone finding me shall kill me."

*"Driven out": the verb of 3:24.*

**15** And YHWH said to him, "Therefore everyone killing Qayin, sevenfold it shall be avenged." And YHWH set for Qayin a sign, so that everyone finding him should not strike him.

*"A sign for Qayin," not "a mark upon Cain": the word is the "signs" of 1:14 and the rainbow (9:12–13); "for" is the word, not "on." Where a sign is on the body the text says so (Ex 13:9, 16). Nothing in the verse says what the sign was or where. "Therefore" is the word of both Hebrew lines; three old translations read "Not so."*

**16** And Qayin went out from before YHWH, and settled in the land of Nod, east of Eden.

*"Nod" is the noun of "wandering" in 4:12 and 4:14; the land is named by the sentence passed in it. The Greek translation kept it as a place-name, and so does every English one.*

**17** And Qayin knew his woman, and she conceived and bore Chanokh; and he was building a city, and he called the name of the city as the name of his son, Chanokh.

**18** And there was born to Chanokh Irad; and Irad begot Mechuyael; and Mechiyyael begot Metushael; and Metushael begot Lemekh.

**19** And Lemekh took for himself two women; the name of the one was Adah, and the name of the second Tzillah.

**20** And Adah bore Yaval; he was father of tent-dweller and livestock.

⸤ *father of dweller of tent and livestock* ⸥

**21** And the name of his brother was Yuval; he was father of every handler of lyre and pipe.

**22** And Tzillah also bore Tuval-Qayin, sharpener of every craftsman of bronze and iron; and the sister of Tuval-Qayin was Na'amah.

⸤ *And Tzillah, also she, bore* ⸥

**23** And Lemekh said to his women, "Adah and Tzillah, hear my voice; women of Lemekh, give ear to my saying: for I have killed a man for my wound, and a boy for my bruise.

⸤ *for a man I have killed for my wound* ⸥

**24** For sevenfold shall Qayin be avenged, and Lemekh seventy and seven."

*"Seventy and seven": a number, with no "-fold" and no verb; "seventy times seven" is the Greek translation's phrase (Matt 18:22).*

**25** And Adam knew his woman again, and she bore a son, and she called his name Shet, "for God has set for me another seed instead of Hevel, for Qayin killed him."

⸤ *And Adam knew again his woman* ⸥

*"Adam": here, for the first time, the letters themselves have no article and are not after a preposition (compare 2:20; 3:17, 21), so the word reads as a name by its consonants; in 4:1 the letters still read "the human." "Shet" and "set" are one pair of letters in the Hebrew, as "Qayin" and "acquired" are in 4:1.*

**26** And to Shet also was born a son, and he called his name Enosh. Then it was begun to call on the name of YHWH.

⸤ *And to Shet, also he, was born a son* ⸥

*"It was begun": a passive with no subject; "men" or "people" in English is supplied. "Enosh" is a common word for a human being (Ps 8:5; Job 7:17) used as a name, as "Adam" is. "Call on the name of YHWH" is the phrase of Abram at his altars (12:8; 13:4) and of YHWH proclaiming his own name (Ex 33:19; 34:5).*

## Chapter 5

**1** This is the book of the begettings of Adam. In the day God created human, in the likeness of God he made him.

⸤ *In the day of God's creating human* ⸥

*"Begettings," not "generations" as ages or eras: the word is built on "bear" and heads what came from the one named, as in 2:4. "Human": the word has no article here or in 5:2, and the pronouns that follow it are "him" and then "them," as in 1:27; the same letters are the name in 5:3–5, where one man lives and dies.*

**2** Male and female he created them, and he blessed them and called their name human, in the day they were created.

⸤ *in the day of their being created* ⸥

*"Called their name human": the pair receives one name, and it is the word for humankind of 1:26, not a man's name given to the species; the ancient translations wrote the name "Adam" here, and every English version follows them with "Adam," "Man" or "Mankind."*

**3** And Adam lived a hundred and thirty years, and begot in his likeness, as his image, and called his name Shet.

⸤ *thirty and a hundred years* ⸥

*"In his likeness, as his image": the two words of 1:26 with their prepositions exchanged; the son stands to Adam as Adam to God in 5:1. The Hebrew names no son in this clause; "a son" is supplied in English.*

**4** And the days of Adam after he begot Shet were eight hundred years, and he begot sons and daughters.

⸤ *after his begetting Shet* ⸥

**5** And all the days of Adam which he lived were nine hundred years and thirty years, and he died.

*The numbers printed here are those of the pointed Hebrew text, which the oldest Latin and Syriac translations share. The second Hebrew line and the oldest Greek translation give other numbers in most lines of this chapter (5:3–31); the full table is in the verse log at 5:3 and the case in the variant table. In all three sets the total is the sum of the two parts. The Hebrew says the smaller number first and repeats "years" after each part; both are kept.*

**6** And Shet lived five years and a hundred years, and begot Enosh.

**7** And Shet lived after he begot Enosh seven years and eight hundred years, and begot sons and daughters.

**8** And all the days of Shet were twelve years and nine hundred years, and he died.

**9** And Enosh lived ninety years, and begot Qeinan.

**10** And Enosh lived after he begot Qeinan fifteen years and eight hundred years, and begot sons and daughters.

⸤ *after his begetting Qeinan* ⸥

**11** And all the days of Enosh were five years and nine hundred years, and he died.

**12** And Qeinan lived seventy years, and begot Mahalal'el.

**13** And Qeinan lived after he begot Mahalal'el forty years and eight hundred years, and begot sons and daughters.

⸤ *after his begetting Mahalal'el* ⸥

**14** And all the days of Qeinan were ten years and nine hundred years, and he died.

**15** And Mahalal'el lived five years and sixty years, and begot Yered.

**16** And Mahalal'el lived after he begot Yered thirty years and eight hundred years, and begot sons and daughters.

⸤ *after his begetting Yered* ⸥

**17** And all the days of Mahalal'el were five and ninety years and eight hundred years, and he died.

**18** And Yered lived two and sixty years and a hundred years, and begot Chanokh.

**19** And Yered lived after he begot Chanokh eight hundred years, and begot sons and daughters.

⸤ *after his begetting Chanokh* ⸥

**20** And all the days of Yered were two and sixty years and nine hundred years, and he died.

**21** And Chanokh lived five and sixty years, and begot Metushelach.

**22** And Chanokh walked about with God after he begot Metushelach three hundred years, and begot sons and daughters.

⸤ *after his begetting Metushelach* ⸥

*"Walked about with God": the verb is the one of 3:8, where it is God walking about in the garden; "with" is the word of 4:1. The phrase is said again of Noach (6:9) and of no one else. The oldest Greek translation rendered it "pleased God" here and in 5:24, and that wording passed into the New Testament (Heb 11:5); the Hebrew has a verb of walking, and "faithfully" is not in it.*

**23** And all the days of Chanokh were five and sixty years and three hundred years.

**24** And Chanokh walked about with God, and he was not, for God took him.

*"He was not" is the phrase said of a person who is gone (Gen 37:30; 42:13, 36; Ps 37:10); "took" is the ordinary verb for taking (2:15; 3:6; 4:11). The verse names no place. Where the Bible tells of a man taken by God and says where, it says so (2 Kgs 2:11, "into the heavens"); "translated" and "transferred" are the Greek translation's word (Heb 11:5), not the Hebrew's.*

**25** And Metushelach lived seven and eighty years and a hundred years, and begot Lemekh.

**26** And Metushelach lived after he begot Lemekh two and eighty years and seven hundred years, and begot sons and daughters.

⸤ *after his begetting Lemekh* ⸥

**27** And all the days of Metushelach were nine and sixty years and nine hundred years, and he died.

**28** And Lemekh lived two and eighty years and a hundred years, and begot a son.

**29** And he called his name Noach, saying, "This one shall comfort us from our work and from the toil of our hands, from the ground which YHWH cursed."

*"Comfort": the verb has the two letters of the name and one more; the name itself has the letters of "rest," a different verb the verse does not use, and the Greek translation rendered "give us rest." "Toil" is the word said to the woman and the man in 3:16–17, its third and last use. "From the ground which YHWH cursed": in 3:17 the ground "is cursed" with no one named; here the speaker names YHWH. The three "from" are parallel in the Hebrew; "concerning" and "because of" are not in it.*

**30** And Lemekh lived after he begot Noach five and ninety years and five hundred years, and begot sons and daughters.

⸤ *after his begetting Noach* ⸥

**31** And all the days of Lemekh were seven and seventy years and seven hundred years, and he died.

**32** And Noach was five hundred years old, and Noach begot Shem, Cham and Yefet.

⸤ *a son of five hundred years* ⸥

*"Five hundred years old" is the Hebrew idiom "son of five hundred years" (7:6; 12:4; 21:5). Noach's line is left open: no "sons and daughters," no total, no death; 9:28–29 closes it in the formula's words.*
