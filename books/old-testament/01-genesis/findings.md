# Genesis — findings

Running conclusions from the study, each tied to the verses that support it and the rows in belief/translation-to-belief.md. Updated as the reading advances; a later verse may confirm, sharpen or complicate any of these. Coverage so far: Genesis 1:1–8:22.

## 1. The text defines its own words

Genesis names the expanse "heavens" (1:8) and the dry ground "earth" (1:10), so "the heavens and the earth" of 1:1 means, by the chapter's own glossary, a beaten-out sheet and dry land. Eden is a garden inside a region, on a real river system, described in the present tense (2:8–14). "Heaven" as the afterlife, "earth" as the planet and Eden as paradise are brought to the text, not found in it.

*Verses:* 1:1, 1:8, 1:10, 2:8, 2:10–14. *Rows:* 1, 5, 6, 18.

*Ch. 3 adds:* the garden keeps its map. The human is sent "to work the ground from which he was taken" (3:23), the phrase of 2:5; the cherubim stand "to the east of the garden" (3:24), which fixes the "east" of 2:8 as a direction. No word for heaven, sky or another world enters the chapter. *Verses:* 3:8, 3:23–24.

*Ch. 4 adds:* the ground (adamah) is the chapter's stage: it is worked (4:2, 12), yields fruit (4:3), receives blood and has a mouth (4:10–11), and is left (4:14). Qayin settles "east of Eden" (4:16), the direction of 3:24, in a land whose name is the chapter's own word for wandering (4:12, 14). The city, the tent, livestock, the lyre and pipe, bronze and iron all enter as common nouns with no origin stated beyond a founder's name (4:17, 20–22). No word for heaven or another world occurs. *Verses:* 4:2–3, 4:10–12, 4:14, 4:16–22. *Rows:* 39.

*Ch. 5 adds:* the ground (adamah) is named once, in Lemekh's speech (5:29), as "the ground which YHWH cursed," the ground of 3:17; no other place-word occurs in the chapter. Chanokh is "taken" (5:24) with no place named: no heavens, no garden, no Eden. The chapter's nouns are years, days, sons, daughters, names. *Verses:* 5:24, 5:29. *Rows:* 44.

*Ch. 6 adds:* the stage is "the earth" (ha-aretz, 6:4, 5, 6, 11 ×2, 12 ×2, 13 ×2, 17 ×2) and "the ground" (ha-adamah, 6:1, 7, 20), the words of 1:10 and 2:5–7; the flood is "waters upon the earth" (6:17) and all flesh is "from under the heavens" (6:17), the phrase of 1:9 for the space beneath the expanse. No word for another world enters; the one place named is the box, measured in cubits (6:15). "Sons of God" (6:2, 4) are named without a place of origin, as the cherubim were in 3:24. *Verses:* 6:1–7, 6:11–17. *Rows:* 47, 60.

*Ch. 7 adds:* the stage is "the earth" (ha-aretz, 7:3, 4, 6, 10, 12, 14, 17 ×2, 18, 19, 21 ×2, 23, 24), "the ground" (ha-adamah, 7:4, 8, 23), "the mountains" (7:19, 20) and "the dry-land" (7:22); the waters come from "the great deep" of 1:2 and through "the windows of the heavens," the shamayim of 1:8 (7:11); the extent of the covering is "under all the heavens" (7:19), the phrase of 1:9 for the space beneath the expanse. No word for another world enters; the box is again the one named place, and it "went upon the face of the waters" (7:18), the four words of 1:2. *Verses:* 7:11, 7:18–22. *Rows:* 65, 67.

*Ch. 8 adds:* the stage is the earth (ha-aretz, 8:1, 3, 7, 9, 11, 13, 14, 17 ×3, 19, 22), the ground (ha-adamah, 8:8, 13, 21), the mountains (8:4, 5) and, for the first time since Eden and Nod, a named region, "the mountains of Ararat" (8:4), a land and kingdom in 2 Kgs 19:37 and Jer 51:27, named here in the plural with no single peak. The earth is given "days" (kol-yemei ha-aretz, 8:22), the phrase that measured each father's life in ch. 5. No word for another world enters; the heavens are named only as the source of rain and the place of the windows (8:2). *Verses:* 8:2, 8:4, 8:22. *Rows:* 79.

## 2. The Hebrew cosmos is structured and populated, but Genesis never makes its inhabitants

A solid *raqia* with water above it; lamps set inside it; birds across its face; a *host* belonging to heavens and earth at the end of the week (2:1); "let us make" spoken with others present (1:26). Yet no day of creation makes any heavenly being. The assumption that "heavens" in 1:1 includes angels is read in from other books.

*Verses:* 1:6–8, 1:14–17, 1:20, 1:26–27, 2:1. *Rows:* 3, 5, 14, 15.

*Ch. 3 adds:* "like one of us" (3:22) is the plural of 1:26 in the narrator's mouth, set against the serpent's "like God" (3:5) with the same word; the cherubim appear with the article, as known figures, and are not made in the chapter (3:24). Two more inhabitants of the structured cosmos, neither created on the page. *Verses:* 3:5, 3:22, 3:24. *Rows:* 4, 14, 27.

*Ch. 4 adds:* nothing is made in the chapter and no inhabitant of the cosmos is named; the only speakers are YHWH and humans. "Everyone finding me" (4:14) and "everyone killing Qayin" (4:15) presuppose other people whose origin the chapter does not give, a gap of the same kind as the host of 2:1. *Verses:* 4:14–15.

*Ch. 5 adds:* God is the only non-human actor: he creates, blesses, names (5:1–2) and takes (5:24); "walked about with God" is said of a man with the verb used of God in 3:8. No made inhabitant of the cosmos is named; the "image" and "likeness" of 1:26 recur as the terms of a father and son (5:3). *Verses:* 5:1–3, 5:22, 5:24. *Rows:* 43.

*Ch. 6 adds:* "the sons of God" (6:2, 4) act in the chapter, seeing, taking and coming in, and are not made in it or anywhere in Genesis; the phrase is that of Job 1:6; 2:1; 38:7, where they are present at the laying of the earth's foundation. The Nefilim "were in the earth" (6:4) with no origin stated. These are the third and fourth inhabitants of the cosmos to appear uncreated (after the host, 2:1, and the cherubim, 3:24). Count: the phrase benei (ha-)Elohim occurs five times in the Bible, two of them here; see row 3. *Verses:* 6:2, 6:4. *Rows:* 3, 47.

*Ch. 7 adds:* no inhabitant of the cosmos acts or is named; the actors are YHWH/Elohim, Noach, the animals and the waters. The structure of ch. 1 is the chapter's machinery: the deep (1:2) has fountains that are split (7:11), the heavens (1:8) have windows that are opened (7:11), so that the waters below and above the expanse (1:6–7) are the two sources named. The rain is the third (7:12). *Verses:* 7:11–12. *Rows:* 65.

*Ch. 8 adds:* the structure of ch. 1 is closed as it was opened: the fountains of the deep and the windows of the heavens are stopped (8:2), and a ruach, the word of 1:2, passes over the earth at God's sending before the waters subside (8:1). No inhabitant of the cosmos acts or is named; the chapter's agents are God, Noach, the raven, the dove and the waters. *Verses:* 8:1–2. *Rows:* 72.

## 3. Genesis 1–2 is written in sanctuary vocabulary, and English loses it

The lamps exist for *mo'adim*, the festival calendar (1:14). *Tzelem* is the word for a cult statue (1:26). The first "holy" is a day (2:3). The human's work in the garden, "serve and guard," is the Levites' formula for sanctuary duty (2:15). No English version carries any of these links. Weight varies: \*tzelem\* is a made image in 15 of 17 uses; the \*avad\*/\*shamar\* pairing for one task occurs six times, five of them sanctuary duty, but each verb alone is an ordinary farm or guard word; \*mo'ed\* is the festival word in Lev 23 but means an appointed time of any kind (Gen 17:21). See Concordance.

*Verses:* 1:14, 1:26–27, 2:3, 2:15. *Rows:* 8, 12, 20.

*Ch. 3 adds:* shamar, the second verb of 2:15, returns as the cherubim's task (3:24), and the cherubim are the figures of the chest and curtains (Ex 25:18–20; 26:31); God "stations" them with the verb behind mishkan (3:24); the tunics and the verb "clothed" are the words of the priests' investiture (3:21; Ex 29:5, 8), though kuttonet is also Joseph's and Tamar's garment and the Concordance rates that echo weak. Count: cherubim outside Gen 3 are sanctuary or throne figures in every use (90 of 91); kuttonet is priestly in 14 of 29, so that echo carries no weight on its own. *Verses:* 3:21, 3:24. *Rows:* 4, 20.

*Ch. 4 adds:* shamar is now in a human mouth, "a keeper of my brother" (4:9), after 2:15 and 3:24; the offerings are minchah, the gift word that the law uses for the grain offering (Lev 2) and that Jacob uses for Esau (32:14), with no altar, no zavach and no blood of an animal in the chapter (Concordance: minchah, representative); "call on the name of YHWH" (4:26) is the phrase of Abram's altars (12:8; 13:4). Count: the sanctuary vocabulary of ch. 4 is two words, minchah and qara be-shem, and both are ordinary outside the sanctuary too; the chapter's one ritual act is named by the tribute word. *Verses:* 4:3–5, 4:9, 4:26. *Rows:* 34, 40.

*Ch. 5 adds:* nothing of the sanctuary vocabulary; the chapter has no offering, altar, priest, holy or keep. Its one ritual-sounding phrase, "walked about with God," is the hithpael of 3:8, used of God in the camp (Deut 23:15) and the tent (2 Sam 7:6–7), and of a person "in" integrity (Ps 101:2); with et, "with," it is said only of Chanokh and Noach (Concordance: hithallekh, representative, 3 of about 64). *Verses:* 5:22, 5:24. *Rows:* 43.

*Ch. 6 adds:* the box is built with the three measure-words of the covenant chest and the temple, "length, breadth, height" (orekh, rochav, qomah: Ex 25:10, 23; 27:1; 1 Kgs 6:2), in cubits (ammah, the unit of Ex 25–27), and is "pitched inside and outside" with the pair of words used of the chest's gold overlay (mi-bayit u-mi-chutz, Ex 25:11; 37:2). Noach is tamim, the word of the offering animal "without blemish" (Lev 1:3; about 50 of 91 uses), and the verb for pitching, kafar, has the consonants of kipper, "make atonement," its only qal use (6:14). Weight: the measure-words and "inside and outside" are ordinary building terms (1 Kgs 7:9 of stone); tamim's majority sense is cultic by count; the kafar/kipper link is a fact of letters, the qal occurring once. The box itself (tevah) is not the sanctuary word aron (Concordance, tevah). *Verses:* 6:9, 6:14–15. *Rows:* 54, 57.

*Ch. 7 adds:* tahor, "clean," has its first use in the Bible at 7:2 (7:2 ×2, 8 ×2; 8:20 ×2), the law's word for the category of Lev 11 and Deut 14 and the word of "pure gold" for the sanctuary's furniture (Ex 25–39, about 30 uses), spoken before any law and never defined in Genesis; the chapter's negative is "not clean," not the law's tame. "In the bone of this day" (7:13) is the dating idiom of the exodus (Ex 12:17, 41, 51), the appointed days (Lev 23:21, 28, 29, 30) and Abraham's circumcision (17:23, 26): 9 of its 12 uses. The seven-day interval (7:4, 10) and "seven seven" (7:2–3) are counts, not the festival word. Weight: tahor's cultic and legal uses are about 72 of 93 (Concordance, representative); the dating idiom is complete at 12. *Verses:* 7:2–4, 7:8, 7:10, 7:13. *Rows:* 62, 63.

*Ch. 8 adds:* the chapter's last three verses are the law's vocabulary in its first uses: "altar" (mizbeach, 8:20; 401 uses), "ascent-offering" (olah, 8:20; 289 uses, the law's and the temple's in about 280), "clean" (tahor, 8:20, after 7:2, 8), "the smell of the soothing" (reach ha-nichoach, 8:21; 43 uses, every one of an offering, the formula of Lev 1–23). Before them, the box's "covering" is mikhseh (8:13), the word for the tent's skin coverings in 15 of its 16 uses; the box "rested" (nuach, 8:4), the verb of Ex 20:11 and of the chest at Num 10:36; and God "spoke… saying" (8:15), the frame of about ninety sections of the law. None of these words is defined in Genesis; each is spoken before the law that fixes it. Weight: olah and nichoach are cultic by distribution (about 280 of 289; 43 of 43); mikhseh 15 of 16; mizbeach is the altar word everywhere; nuach is the ordinary verb of coming to rest (Concordance, representative). *Verses:* 8:4, 8:13, 8:15, 8:20–21. *Rows:* 62, 73, 74, 75.

## 4. Humans and animals stand closer in the Hebrew than in English

"Living soul" is one phrase for fish, beasts and the human (1:20–30; 2:7). *Adam* is collective, male and female (1:26–27). The first blessing goes to fish and birds (1:22). What sets humans apart is image, likeness and rule, not soul. In ch. 2 the animals are formed from the same ground as the human (2:19) and are "living soul" as he names them; what he does that they do not is name, as God named in ch. 1. The woman is built from his side, a half, and greeted with a kinship formula, not an anatomy.

*Verses:* 1:20–22, 1:24, 1:26–28, 1:30, 2:7, 2:19–23. *Rows:* 9, 11, 12, 13, 22, 23.

*Ch. 3 adds:* the serpent is "of the living-things of the field which YHWH God made" (3:1), the class the human named in 2:19–20, and it is sentenced as an animal, "from every beast and every living-thing of the field" (3:14). The woman and the man receive the same word, itzavon, "toil" (3:16–17); the curse falls on the serpent and the ground, not on either human (3:14, 17). The man is "taken" from the ground (3:19, 23) with the verb used of the woman taken from him (2:22–23). *Verses:* 3:1, 3:14, 3:16–17, 3:19, 3:23. *Rows:* 25, 30.

*Ch. 4 adds:* the first humans born are introduced by occupation, shepherd of flock and worker of ground (4:2), and Qayin is cursed "from the ground" in the wording used of the serpent, "from every beast" (3:14; 4:11); the sentence on him uses two participles of motion (na va-nad) elsewhere said of a reed, a bird and a staggering earth (4:12). Hevel's name is a common noun (breath), Enosh's is the poetic word for a human being (4:26), Adam's the word for humankind, first read as a name by its consonants at 4:25. *Verses:* 4:2, 4:11–12, 4:25–26. *Rows:* 11, 40.

*Ch. 5 adds:* "male and female" (5:2) is the pair of words used of the animals entering the ark (6:19; 7:3, 9, 16), restated of the humans from 1:27. The human's distinction in the chapter is carried by "likeness" and "image" (5:1, 3), the terms of 1:26, not by any word for soul or breath, which do not occur. "Lived" (chayah) is said of each father, the verb behind the "living" that 1:20–30 and 2:7 share with the animals; "died" (mut) closes eight lines, the verb of 2:17. *Verses:* 5:1–5, 5:8, 5:11. *Rows:* 42.

*Ch. 6 adds:* "all flesh" (kol-basar, 6:12, 13, 17, 19) is one phrase for the human and the animals, defined in 6:17 as everything "in which is spirit of life" (ruach chayyim), and in 6:19 as what enters the box "male and female" (the pair of 1:27; 5:2); the flood "ruins all flesh" and "all which is in the earth shall expire" (6:17) with no word that singles out the human. "The spirit" God calls "mine" in 6:3 is by name in every animal in 6:17 (and 7:15, 22). Food is one word for both, "for you and for them" (6:21), the okhlah of 1:29–30. Against this, 6:5–7 speak of the human alone (ha-adam seven times) and the wiping-out of 6:7 lists the human first. Count: four uses of kol-basar in the chapter, two with the animals stated in them. *Verses:* 6:3, 6:7, 6:12–13, 6:17–21. *Rows:* 56, 60.

*Ch. 7 adds:* the animals are paired with the human words: "a man and his woman" (ish ve-ishto, 7:2 ×2), the words of 2:23–24 and 6:18, where 7:3, 9, 16 have "male and female"; the breath of 2:7, "in his nostrils," is said at 7:22 of "all which was in the dry-land," with the two nouns of 2:7 and 6:17 joined (nishmat-ruach chayyim); "all flesh" is listed at 7:21 as flyer, beast, living-thing, swarmer and, last, "all the human," and one verb, "expired," covers the five; "died" (7:22) is said of the same. Against this, 7:1 says "righteous" of Noach alone, and 7:23's list sets the human first. Count: of the chapter's five catalogues (7:2–3, 8, 14, 21, 23) the human stands in two, last in one and first in one. *Verses:* 7:2–3, 7:21–23. *Rows:* 61, 68, 70.

*Ch. 8 adds:* God "remembered" Noach, every living-thing and every beast with one verb and three et (8:1); the blessing of 1:22 to the fish and flyers, "be fruitful and multiply," is said of the animals leaving the box with "swarm" before it (8:17), and to the human only in 9:1; the animals leave "by their families" (8:19), the word the next chapters use for the peoples (10:5–32); and YHWH's resolve names "all living" (kol-chai, 8:21, the phrase of 3:20) as what he will not smite again, the human and the animals under one word. Against this, the heart whose forming is bad is the human's alone (8:21), the grounds of the curse are "on account of the human" (8:21), and the altar's animals are taken, not blessed (8:20). Count: three verbs in the chapter have the human and the animals together as object or subject (remembered, 8:1; bring out, 8:17; smite, 8:21). *Verses:* 8:1, 8:17–21. *Rows:* 71, 74.

## 5. Belief was shaped three ways, and only the first is mistranslation

**(a) KJV smoothing or error:** singular "heaven"; "moved" for hovering; "whales" for sea-monsters; "rib" for side; "the first day" for "day one"; "soul" for the human alone; "fowl" produced by the waters; "angels" in Ps 8:5. *Rows:* 1, 2, 9, 10, 23.

**(b) KJV right, English drifted:** "seasons," "evil," "man," "dominion," "help meet" (fused into "helpmate"); and "firmament," where the 1611 word is now more literal than the modern "expanse." *Rows:* 5, 7, 8, 11, 13, 19, 22.

**(c) KJV right, belief arrived by another route:** paradise (via Greek and the New Testament); "Jehovah" (a hybrid of consonants and borrowed vowels); the "us" puzzle, which exists only because a singular heaven leaves no one in the room; "surely die" read as a clock rather than a sentence; "the two" in "one flesh," which is in the Greek and the Gospels but not the Hebrew of Genesis. *Rows:* 14, 17, 18, 21, 24.

Most of what shaped belief was a faithful word read with a later meaning, or a verse read without the other verses that use the same phrase.

*Ch. 3 adds:* (a) none new in KJV; the modern "crush / strike" (NIV) and capital "He" (NASB) at 3:15 are the chapter's smoothing cases, and modern versions split "toil" into "pain" for her and "toil" for him. (b) "subtil" (now faint, then crafty) and "sorrow" (now grief, then also hardship). (c) "serpent" read through Rev 12:9; "Eve" through Latin, which breaks the name from the verse's own gloss; "Adam" at 3:17 and 3:21 on a vowel, as at 2:20. *Rows:* 25, 26, 28, 29, 30, 32.

*Ch. 4 adds:* (a) KJV: "from the LORD" for "with" (4:1), "had respect unto" for "looked to" (4:4), "accepted" for "a lifting" (4:7), "a mark upon Cain" for "a sign for Qayin" (4:15), "punishment" for "iniquity" (4:13), "men" supplied at 4:26; modern versions share all but the first. (b) "offering" (exact, but heard as sacrifice) and "lieth" (now also "deceives"). (c) "Nod" and "Adam" at 4:1 by transliteration and by a name read where the letters have the article; "seventy times seven" at 4:24 from the Greek and Matt 18:22. *Rows:* 33–40.

*Ch. 5 adds:* (a) KJV: "generations" for "begettings" (5:1), where the English word has drifted to mean ages; "concerning… because of" for the three parallel "from" (5:29). (b) "called their name Adam" (5:2): the KJV transliterates where the Hebrew has the common noun of 1:26, and the modern "Man," "Mankind," "Humankind" each decide what the KJV left ambiguous. (c) "pleased God" and "translated" (Heb 11:5) from the Greek of 5:22–24, which English readers carry back to the Hebrew "walked… took"; and the chapter's numbers, which every version prints from the MT without a note, so the English reader does not see that the Samaritan and Greek lines carry two other worked chronologies. *Rows:* 41–46.

*Ch. 6 adds:* (a) KJV: "giants" (6:4, the Greek word), "strive" (6:3, one root chosen where the vowels fix none), "destroy" for "wipe out" (6:7) and for "ruin" (6:13, 17), "rooms" for "nests" (6:14), "window" for a word of one use (6:16), "breath of life" for a different word than 2:7 (6:17); the modern versions share all but "giants" and "strive," and add "roof" (6:16). (b) "imagination" (then a devising, now fancy, 6:5), "repented" (then changed his mind, now turned from sin, 6:6), "grace" (then favor, now a doctrine, 6:8), "perfect" (then whole, now flawless, 6:9), "generations" (then also an age, 6:9). (c) "ark" for two Hebrew words through Greek kibotos and Latin arca (6:14; Ex 25:10); "angels" at 6:2 in one Greek copy; "sons of God" read as the Sethite line, which needs no mistranslation and arises when row 1's single heaven leaves the court no room (row 3). The chapter's split of one verb into "corrupt" and "destroy" (6:11–17) is in every version, old and new. *Rows:* 47–60.

*Ch. 7 adds:* (a) KJV: "was destroyed" (7:23), a passive where the pointing, the object marker and the three old translations read the active; "destroy" for "wipe out" (7:4, 23, as 6:7); "every bird of every sort" (7:14) for two bare nouns. The modern versions share "destroy"/"blot out" and add "seven pairs" (7:2–3, five of six), which the Hebrew's "seven seven" does not state, and "floodgates" (7:11, three of six). (b) "by sevens" (exact, now unclear), "the selfsame day" (exact, now archaic), "prevailed" (exact, kept by three of six), "the windows of heaven" (exact in the noun, singular in "heaven"). (c) "the breath of life" at 7:22, read as 2:7's phrase because the KJV used the same English at 2:7 and 6:17, where the Hebrew has three different phrases; "dry land" at 7:22 read as 1:9's word; "ark" throughout (row 57); "clean" heard as hygiene, a category word read as a common one (the row 7 pattern). *Rows:* 61–70.

*Ch. 8 adds:* (a) KJV: "burnt offerings" (8:20; fire supplied, through Latin holocaustum), "sweet savour" (8:21; taste for rest), "while the earth remaineth" (8:22; a verb supplied); the modern versions share the first two ("pleasing aroma") and add "to Himself" for "to his heart" (NASB, JPS), "from childhood" (NIV), "doom" (JPS). (b) "remembered" (exact; now heard as recalling what was forgotten), "asswaged" (exact, archaic), "stayed" for waited, "imagination" again (row 50). (c) "Mount Ararat" as one peak, while all six print "the mountains of Ararat" and the Hebrew names a region; the "olive branch" of the Vulgate (ramum) where the Hebrew has one leaf; "the rainbow" read back into 8:21–22 from 9:12–17; the dove's "rest" (8:9) and the smell's "rest" (8:21) read as two things because English has no one word for the root of 8:4. *Rows:* 71–79.

## 6. The Hebrew text is stable; the vowels are not the text

Dead Sea Scrolls, Samaritan Pentateuch and Masoretic Text agree almost letter for letter wherever all three are present. The DSS attest nearly all of ch. 1 but of ch. 2 only 2:1–3, 6–7 and 14–19; for the rest of ch. 2 the Hebrew witnesses are SP and MT alone, which agree across 1:1–2:25 except for the SP's "sixth" at 2:2. The Greek harmonizes and adds (1:7, 1:8, 1:9, 1:14, 1:20, 2:4); the harder Hebrew reading wins in every case here, with Deut 32:8 on file as the case where the method cuts against the MT. The LXX also harmonizes 2:18 to "let us make" (the Hebrew is singular) and adds "two" in 2:24. The medieval vowel points decide three things of weight: *bereshit* (no article) and the name YHWH (pointed with another word's vowels), and whether \*adam\* in 2:20 is "the human" or the name "Adam," since the article there is a vowel.

*Verses:* 1:1, 1:7–9, 1:14, 1:20, 2:2, 2:4, 2:18, 2:20, 2:24. *See:* Method → Judging variants. *Rows:* 17, 24.

*Ch. 3 adds:* the DSS attest only 3:1–2 (4QGen-k) and 3:11–14 (1QGen); for the rest SP and MT are the Hebrew witnesses, and they agree throughout; at 3:16 the LXX and Syriac read "return" for "desire," one letter apart, and the SP was at first reported with them but on checking (2026-10-24) reads "desire" with the MT, so the two Hebrew lines agree. The LXX smooths at 3:1, 6, 17 and 24 and the MT is kept in each. Vowel-dependent in ch. 3: "shrewd" against "naked" (3:1, consonants of 2:25); "he" in 3:15 (consonants read "she" in 3:12, 20); le-adam without the article in 3:17 and 3:21, as 2:20. *Verses:* 3:1, 3:6, 3:15, 3:16, 3:17, 3:21, 3:24. *Rows:* 26, 28, 31.

*Ch. 4 adds:* the one scroll listed for the chapter, 4QGen-b, is given as 4:2–4 or 4:2–11 and is reported as matching the MT where legible, plates not inspected. The SP consonants were compared with the MT in every verse of ch. 4 (2026-10-24, one online edition): they differ in sense at 4:8 (SP adds "let us go to the field," with LXX, Syriac and Vulgate), 4:10 (singular "crying"), 4:25 ("he called" for "she called") and 4:26 ("he began" for "it was begun"); the rest are spellings. The LXX reads differently at 4:1 ("through God"), 4:7 (the whole first half), 4:13 ("forgiven"), 4:15 ("not so"), 4:24 ("seventy times seven") and 4:26 ("hoped"). ## 7. Genesis 3 is told in common nouns and ordinary verbs; the technical vocabulary later attached to it is absent from the chapter

## 9. Genesis 5 is one formula said ten times, and its departures are countable

The formula has three lines: "And X lived N years and begot Y" (5:3, 6, 9, 12, 15, 18, 21, 25, 28); "And X lived after his begetting Y N years and begot sons and daughters" (5:7, 10, 13, 16, 19, 26, 30); "And all the days of X were N years, and he died" (5:8, 11, 14, 17, 20, 27, 31). Thirty-two verses, of which twenty-one (5:6–21, 25–27, 30–31) are the formula without change. The departures: Adam's line (5:3–5) has "begot in his likeness, as his image" with no object, names the son, has "the days of Adam… were" for "lived" in the second line, "which he lived" in the third, and the hundreds before the units; Chanokh's (5:22–24) has "walked about with God" for "lived" in the second line, a singular "was" and no "and he died" in the third, and a fourth clause, "walked about with God, and he was not, for God took him"; Lemekh's (5:28–29) has "begot a son" and names him with "saying" and a reason; Noach's (5:32) has "was a son of five hundred years" for "lived," three sons, and no second or third line. The framing verses 5:1–2 and, inside the lines, 5:24 and 5:29 are the only verses without a number of years. The MT has a singular "was" with "all the days" at 5:23 and 5:31 and the plural elsewhere; the SP has the singular at 5:23 only. Weight: the counts above are complete for the chapter and checkable in any text; the finding states where the formula varies, not why.

*Verses:* 5:1–32. *Rows:* 41, 43, 44, 45, 46.

*Ch. 6 adds:* the toledot heading recurs (6:9), the third of eleven, and for the first time what follows it is not a list of begettings but a sentence about the man named, his three sons restated from 5:32 (6:10), and the earth; the formula of ch. 5 ("lived… begot… died") does not occur. The end is told three times with three verbs (7:21 expired, 7:22 died, 7:23 wiped out), and the third repeats 6:7's catalogue word for word. *Verses:* 7:1–24.

*Ch. 8 adds:* the date formula of 7:11 recurs four times (8:4, 5, 13, 14), with the year stated only at 8:13 (month and day; month and day; year, month and day; month and day), and the chapter's intervals are counted as ch. 7's were: a hundred and fifty days (8:3), forty days (8:6), seven days twice (8:10, 12). The command to go out (8:16–17) and its execution (8:18–19) are the pair of the command to come in (7:1–3) and its execution (7:7–9, 13–16), with the same persons in the same order and the catalogue in a new order. The dove's three sendings have one frame, "he sent the dove… and the dove" (8:8–9, 10–11, 12), the third ending in a negative. YHWH's speech (8:21–22) has two parallel clauses with one idiom ("I will not add… again") and closes on eight nouns in pairs. *Verses:* 8:3–14, 8:16–22.

## 10. Genesis 6 states the undoing of Genesis 1 in the words of Genesis 1, and names the earth's ruin and God's act with one verb

The sentences of 6:5–7 and 6:11–13 are the sentences of ch. 1 with the predicate changed. "And God saw… that good" (1:4, 10, 12, 18, 21, 25, 31) becomes "and YHWH saw that great was the badness" (6:5) and "and God saw the earth, and behold, it was ruined" (6:12), the last on the frame of 1:31 ("and God saw all that he had made, and behold, good very"). "The human whom I created" (6:7) is 1:27 ("God created the human"); the things to be wiped out are listed in 1:24–28's words and order (human, beast, creeper, flyer of the heavens, 6:7); the things to be kept alive are listed in 1:20–25's words with its refrain "to its kind" (6:20) and 1:27's "male and female" (6:19); their food is 1:29–30's "for food" (6:21). The verb of the ruin is one: shachat stands five times in 6:11–17, for the earth's state (niphal, 6:11, 12), for what all flesh did to its way (hiphil, 6:12), and for what God announces he is doing and will do (hiphil 6:13; piel 6:17); the English versions split it into "corrupt" and "destroy" at the same place in every version, old and new. Beside it the chapter uses two other verbs for the act, "wipe out" (machah, 6:7, the dish-and-book verb) and "expire" (gava, 6:17), and never "kill," "punish" or "judge." Weight: the ch. 1 recurrences are counted in Finding 8 (twenty-three items for ch. 1–6); shachat's five uses are complete for the chapter and the verb's three stems are tabulated in the Concordance; the finding states which words recur and with what predicates, not why.

*Verses:* 6:5–7, 6:11–13, 6:17, 6:19–21. *Rows:* 50, 51, 55, 56, 60.

*Ch. 7 adds:* the undoing continues in ch. 1's words. The dry ground that was "seen" when the waters were gathered (1:9) is covered (7:19, 20), though by a different noun for "dry" at 7:22 (row 67); the deep of 1:2 is split open and the heavens of 1:8 opened (7:11); the box "went upon the face of the waters" (7:18) where the spirit "hovered upon the face of the waters" (1:2); the waters "multiplied" (7:17, 18), the verb of the blessing on fish, flyer and human (1:22, 28); the creeper that creeps and the swarmer that swarms (1:20–26) are named by those words as they expire (7:14, 21); and the breath of 2:7 goes out of "all which was in the dry-land" (7:22). The chapter's verb for the waters is one, gavar, "prevail" (7:18, 19, 20, 24), the root of the "mighty ones" of 6:4, said of water nowhere else (Concordance, 4 of 25). shachat does not recur; the verbs are "wipe out" (7:4, 23 ×2, the verb of 6:7), "expire" (7:21, the verb of 6:17) and "die" (7:22). Weight: the recurrences are counted in Finding 8 (forty-four items for ch. 1–7); gavar's four uses are complete for the chapter. *Verses:* 7:11, 7:17–23. *Rows:* 64, 66, 67, 68.

*Ch. 8 adds:* the restoring is told in ch. 1's words as the undoing was. The ruach passes over the earth where it hovered over the waters (8:1; 1:2); the heads of the mountains "were seen" (nir'u, 8:5) with the niphal of 1:9 ("let the dry be seen," tera'eh); "the earth dried up" (yaveshah ha-aretz, 8:14) with the root of 1:9–10's yabbashah, the "dry" God named "Earth"; the land animals "swarm" (8:17) with the verb of the waters on day five (1:20); "be fruitful and multiply" (8:17) is the blessing of 1:22, 28; "day and night" (8:22) are the nouns of 1:5; "seed" (8:22) the word of 1:11; and "shall not cease" (8:22) negates the verb of 2:2–3. shachat and machah do not recur; the verb for the flood in YHWH's mouth is "smite" (8:21). Weight: the recurrences are counted in Finding 8 (sixty-seven items for ch. 1–8); the ra'ah-niphal and yavesh/yabbashah links are two words each, both checkable in the Concordance's charav/yavesh entry and the 1:9 log. *Verses:* 8:1, 8:5, 8:14, 8:17, 8:22. *Rows:* 72, 79.

## 11. Genesis 7 tells its entry twice and its lists five times, and the memberships differ each time

The chapter has five catalogues of creatures, and no two have the same members or order. 7:2–3: the clean beast, the beast not clean, the flyer of the heavens (three classes, with "seven seven" and "two"). 7:8: the clean beast, the beast not clean, the flyer, all which creeps upon the ground (four). 7:14: the living-thing, the beast, the creeper that creeps, the flyer, "every bird, every wing" (four with a coda, each "to its kind"). 7:21: the flyer, the beast, the living-thing, the swarmer that swarms, all the human (five, the human last). 7:23: human, beast, creeper, flyer of the heavens (four, the human first; the list of 6:7 word for word). The clean/not-clean division stands in two of the five (7:2–3, 8) and nowhere else in Genesis before 8:20; "living-thing" (chayyah) stands in two (7:14, 21); the swarmer in one (7:21); the human in two (7:21, 23). The entry of the persons is told twice (7:7; 7:13) and of the animals twice (7:8–9; 7:14–16), the second telling fuller in each case; the dating of the flood is given twice (7:6; 7:11) and the interval of seven days twice (7:4; 7:10). Weight: the counts are complete for the chapter and checkable in any text; the finding states which lists differ and how, not why.

*Verses:* 7:2–3, 7:6–16, 7:21–23. *Rows:* 61, 62, 63, 70.

*Ch. 8 adds:* the catalogue appears four more times, again with different members and orders: 8:1 (living-thing, beast); 8:17 (living-thing: flyer, beast, creeper that creeps); 8:19 (living-thing, creeper, flyer, all that moves); 8:20 (clean beast, clean flyer). With ch. 7's five, nine lists in two chapters, no two alike; the beast is in eight of the nine (all but 8:19), the flyer in eight (all but 8:1), the living-thing in five, the creeper in five, the swarmer in one, the human in two. The exit is told twice (command 8:16–17; execution 8:18–19) as the entry was told twice. *Verses:* 8:1, 8:16–20.

## 12. Genesis 8 undoes Genesis 7 clause by clause, with the same nouns and the opposite verbs

Fourteen clauses of ch. 7 have a counterpart in ch. 8 that keeps the noun and reverses the verb or the direction. The fountains of the deep "were split" (7:11) and "were stopped" (8:2); the windows of the heavens "were opened" (7:11) and "were stopped" (8:2); "the rain was upon the earth" (7:12) and "the rain from the heavens was restrained" (8:2); the waters "multiplied" and "prevailed" (7:17–24) and "subsided," "returned," "decreased," were "going and decreasing" (8:1, 3, 5); "the mountains were covered" (7:19–20) and "the heads of the mountains were seen" (8:5); the waters "lifted the box, and it rose" (7:17) and "the box rested" (8:4); the waters prevailed "fifty and a hundred days" (7:24) and decreased "at the end of fifty and a hundred days" (8:3); "YHWH shut behind him" (7:16) and "Noach opened the window" (8:6); "Come, you and all your house, into the box" (7:1) and "Go out from the box, you and your woman…" (8:16); "Noach came, and his sons and his woman and the women of his sons with him, into the box" (7:7) and "Noach went out, and his sons and his woman and the women of his sons with him" (8:18); the catalogue "came to Noach, into the box" (7:14–15) and "went out from the box" (8:19); "all flesh expired" (7:21) and "they shall swarm… be fruitful and multiply" (8:17); "I will wipe out all the standing which I made" (7:4) and "I will not add again to smite all living, as I have done" (8:21); the flood begins on the seventeenth of the second month (7:11) and the earth is dry on the twenty-seventh of the second month (8:14). Weight: the fourteen pairs are listed above and are complete for the two chapters by hand; the verbs on the ch. 8 side reverse the ch. 7 verb in every pair, and five of them (subside, stop, restrain, decrease, rest) are first uses in the book; the nouns are ch. 7's in every pair. The finding states which clauses answer which, not why.

*Verses:* 7:1–24; 8:1–21. *Rows:* 71, 73, 77.
