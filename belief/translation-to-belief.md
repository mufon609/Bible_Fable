# Translation choice → belief

Each row is one place where an English rendering, chiefly KJV, permits a reading the Hebrew does not state. Each row states what the English words allow; a claim about what readers in fact believed appears only with a citation. Rows are added as the study reaches them.

| 1 | שָׁמַיִם *shamayim* | Gen 1:1 (also 1:8, 1:20) | One plural/dual word for sky, cosmos, and God's dwelling; no singular exists. In Gen 1 it is the space birds fly through. | "heaven" (singular) | "Heaven" as a single place, the afterlife destination, distinct from the sky. The layered, populated cosmos of Deut 10:14 ("heaven of heavens") and 2 Cor 12:2 ("third heaven") drops out of view. | NIV, ESV, NASB, NRSV: "the heavens." KJV itself in Gen 2:1, 2:4. |
| 2 | אֱלֹהִים *elohim* | Ps 8:5 | Man made "a little lower than *elohim*" (God, or the divine beings). | "a little lower than the angels" (following the Greek LXX, which Heb 2:7 quotes) | This permits a three-tier order, God, angels, man, with a class between. The Hebrew line places man just under elohim and names no separate angelic class. | NASB, NRSV, JPS: "God" / "divine beings"; ESV: "heavenly beings"; NIV: "the angels" (keeps KJV). |
| 3 | בְּנֵי הָאֱלֹהִים *bene ha-elohim* | Gen 6:2, 6:4; Job 1:6, 2:1, 38:7 | Members of God's court. Job 38:7 has them shouting at creation, before any humans exist; Ps 82:1 and 1 Kgs 22:19 show the same assembly around the throne. | "sons of God" (literal, kept) | The words are right, but with row 1's single "heaven" there is no realm for them to inhabit, so readers settle on humans (the Sethite line) or an undefined "angels." Job 38:7 settles the question the text asks. | All keep "sons of God"; NRSV footnotes "heavenly beings"; JPS: "divine beings." |
| 4 | כְּרֻבִים *keruvim* | Gen 3:24; Ezek 1, 10; Ex 25:18–20 | Plural of *keruv*: winged guardian hybrids set at the garden and over the ark; Ezekiel's have four faces. | "cherubims" (double plural; *-im* is already plural) | The double plural tells the English reader nothing about the creature except that the word is foreign; its form is left to be supplied from outside the verse. The Hebrew supplies it: winged guardian hybrids (Ex 25:18–20; Ezek 1, 10). | Modern versions: "cherubim." |
| 5 | רָקִיעַ raqia | Gen 1:6–8, 1:14–17, 1:20; Ezek 1:22; Ps 19:1 | A beaten-out sheet (root raqa, to hammer thin: Ex 39:3, Job 37:18). Named shamayim in 1:8; holds back waters above it (1:7); the lights are set in it (1:17); Ezekiel sees it as a floor under the throne (Ezek 1:22, 26). | "firmament" (from Latin firmamentum, from LXX stereōma, "firm thing") | Reverse case. KJV preserved the solid sense; English readers for three centuries inherited the Hebrew picture of a hard sky with water above it, and the word became a byword for that picture. Modern versions' "expanse" (ESV, NASB, JPS) was chosen to leave the sky's substance open, which lets readers assume the Hebrew is vaguer than it is. The belief that shifted is modern: that Genesis 1 is silent on sky structure. | NRSV "dome," NIV "vault" keep the sense. KJV "firmament" is closer than ESV/NASB "expanse." |
| 6 | אֶרֶץ eretz | Gen 1:1, 1:10, and \~2,500 further uses | Land, ground, territory. Gen 1:10 defines it as the dry ground that appeared when the waters gathered. The same word is "the land of Egypt," "the land of Canaan." No Hebrew word means the planet. | "earth" / "Earth" (capitalized in 1:10); elsewhere "land" by context | "The earth" as the globe. Readers bring the planet to 1:1 and to every later "all the earth" (the flood, Gen 7:19; "the whole earth" at Babel, 11:1), where the Hebrew word is the one used for a country. The choice between "earth" and "land" in each verse is the translator's, and English readers cannot see that it is one word. | None consistently; NIV uses "land" in 1:10 and 1:12. All versions alternate earth/land by context. |
| 7 | מִין min | Gen 1:11–12, 21, 24–25; 6:20; 7:14; Lev 11; Deut 14 | Kind, sort. In Gen 1 it states that plants and animals reproduce their own sort ("seed in it," "to its kind"). In Lev 11 the same word groups ravens, hawks and bats together as "kinds" of flyers: an everyday category word, not a taxonomic rank. | "after his kind" / "after their kind" | "Kind" as a fixed biological unit with boundaries that cannot be crossed ("fixity of species," later "baramin"). The Hebrew phrase is about reproduction, true to type; it does not state whether kinds are fixed. KJV's wording is faithful; the belief grew from reading a category word as a technical one. | ESV "according to its kind" matches. NRSV/JPS "of every kind" shifts the sense to variety, further from the Hebrew. |
| 8 | מוֹעֲדִים mo'adim | Gen 1:14; Lev 23:2, 4, 37, 44; Num 28–29; Ps 104:19 | Appointed times, fixed meetings. The word for the festival calendar (Lev 23 lists "the appointed times of YHWH": Passover, Weeks, Trumpets, Atonement, Booths) and for the tent of meeting. Gen 1:14 says the lamps exist to mark that calendar. The four seasons have their own vocabulary (Gen 8:22). | "seasons" (accurate in 1611, when a season meant an appointed time) | The lights as markers of weather seasons, a nature statement. The Hebrew ties the sun and moon to worship from day four: the lamps are set to time the feasts. Readers of "seasons" miss that Gen 1 and Lev 23 use one word, and that Ps 104:19 ("the moon for appointed times") is the same claim. The drift is in English, not in KJV's choice. | NIV 2011 "sacred times," JPS "set times." ESV, NASB, NRSV keep "seasons." Of 223 uses, none is a weather season; 41 are festival times, 22 a set time of any kind (Concordance). |
| 9 | נֶפֶשׁ חַיָּה nefesh chayyah | Gen 1:20, 21, 24, 30; 2:7; 2:19; 9:10 | "Living soul" / "living being." nefesh is the throat, the breath, the life in a body, a person. One phrase, applied to sea creatures (1:20–21), land animals (1:24, 30; 2:19) and Adam (2:7) without any change in wording. | Animals: "the moving creature that hath life" (1:20), "living creature" (1:21, 24; 2:19), "life" (1:30). Adam: "a living soul" (2:7). | Humans have souls; animals do not. The English reader meets "soul" for the first time at Adam and never sees it on an animal, so the word appears to mark a human distinction. In the Hebrew, Adam becomes exactly what the fish already were. Whatever sets humans apart in Gen 1–2 is carried by other words (image, likeness, rule), not by nefesh. Modern versions fixed the animals' side ("living creatures") but kept "being" for Adam, so the split survives in a milder form. | None renders all occurrences alike. JPS comes closest: "living creatures" / "living being." |
| 10 | תַּנִּין tannin | Gen 1:21; Ex 7:9–12; Deut 32:33; Ps 74:13, 91:13, 148:7; Isa 27:1, 51:9; Job 7:12; Ezek 29:3, 32:2 | A serpent or sea-monster. Where God breaks or pierces it, the text names the enemy: Pharaoh (Ezek 29:3, 32:2); Egypt at the sea, since Isa 51:9–10 and Ps 74:13–14 run straight on to the exodus; a creature of the sea in Isa 27:1 and Job 7:12. Ps 148:7 summons tanninim to praise with the rest of creation. In Gen 1:21 it is a creature God made on day five, with the verb bara, and blessed in 1:22. | "whales" (1:21); elsewhere "dragon(s)" (Ps 74:13, Isa 27:1, 51:9, Ezek 29:3), "serpent" (Ex 7), "sea monsters" (Lam 4:3) | Readers of KJV cannot see that the "whales" of creation and the "dragon" God slays in Isa 51:9 are one word. The Hebrew places the monster inside the created order from the start, which changes how the later combat passages read. Modern "great sea creatures" (ESV, NASB) keeps the domestication; NRSV/JPS "sea monsters" restores the link. | NRSV, JPS: "great sea monsters." |
| 11 | אָדָם adam | Gen 1:26–27; 2:5–8; 5:1–2; and as a common noun throughout | Human, humankind; built on adamah, ground. In 1:26 it has no article and takes plural pronouns ("let them rule"); in 1:27 "him" and "them" both refer to it; in 5:2 God names "them" adam. It becomes a personal name only later in ch. 2–5, and even there the article (ha-adam, "the human") stays on it through most of the story. | "man" (1:26–27), "Adam" from 2:19 on | A male individual created first, with woman secondary. The Hebrew of 1:26–27 creates a collective, male and female at once. "Man" was a collective in 1611 English and has since narrowed; and KJV's switch to the name "Adam" in 2:19 is the translators' choice (the Hebrew still has the article there). | NIV "mankind," NASB 2020 "mankind," NRSV "humankind." ESV and JPS keep "man." |
| 12 | צֶלֶם tzelem | Gen 1:26–27; 5:3; 9:6; Num 33:52; 1 Sam 6:5; 2 Kgs 11:18; Ezek 7:20; Amos 5:26 | Image, statue, carved figure. Outside Genesis it is almost always a cult image: the statues of a god that Israel is told to destroy. Gen 9:6 grounds the ban on murder in it. The word is concrete. | "image" (1:26); "images" of idols elsewhere (Num 33:52, 2 Kgs 11:18) | "Image of God" as an inner, non-physical quality (reason, conscience, a soul). KJV's word is right, but because KJV renders the same word "images" when it means idols, readers never connect the two, and cannot see that 1:26 runs from image straight to rule in one sentence. The abstraction is the reader's, not the translator's. | All keep "image." None shows the idol-word link in the text itself. |
| 13 | רָדָה radah / כָּבַשׁ kavash | Gen 1:26, 28; Lev 25:43, 46, 53; Num 32:22, 29; 1 Kgs 4:24; Ps 72:8; Jer 34:11 | radah: to rule, hold sway; Lev 25 uses it of a master over a servant and forbids doing it harshly. kavash: to subdue, tread down; used of conquering land (Num 32) and forcing people into slavery (Jer 34:11). Both are strong, political words for a ruler over subjects. | "have dominion" / "subdue" | "Dominion" permits a reading in which the relation to the earth is a standing right rather than an office held under conditions. The Hebrew is accurate in KJV; the words are royal, and what they license depends on how the Bible pictures a king who rules (Ps 72, Lev 25), not on the words alone. A translation issue only in that "dominion" has no everyday use in English and "rule" does. | NIV, NASB, JPS: "rule." ESV, NRSV keep "have dominion." |
| 14 | נַעֲשֶׂה na'aseh | Gen 1:26; 3:22; 11:7; Isa 6:8 | "Let us make": first-person plural. Hebrew verbs have no royal plural; kings in the Bible speak in the singular. The making itself (1:27) is singular. The other three uses of this divine "us" all occur where others are present: in Isa 6 the seraphim; in Gen 3:22 and 11:7 the same court (cf. Job 38:7, the sons of God at creation). | "Let us make man" | Every version keeps the plural, so no translation shaped this. What shaped belief is row 1: with "heaven" as a single place and no visible court, English readers have no one for God to be addressing, so "us" must be explained from outside the chapter. With the Hebrew cosmos in view, the chapter already supplied an audience. | All: "let us make." |
| 15 | צָבָא tzava | Gen 2:1 (and in "YHWH of hosts" \~250 times) | Army, host, ordered company. In 2:1 it belongs to the heavens and the earth together ("their host") and is said to be finished. Ch. 1 narrated no making of any heavenly population; 2:1 asserts one exists without saying how. The word elsewhere names stars and the beings around the throne alike. | "host" | Two opposite beliefs, both from reading beyond the verse. (a) "The heavens" of 1:1 included the angels, so Gen 1 covers their creation. The chapter's own definition of shamayim (1:8) is the expanse, and no day makes its inhabitants. (b) The host is only the stars (NASB 2020 "heavenly lights"), so Gen 1–2 says nothing of other beings. The word does not choose; it is the same word in 1 Kgs 22:19 for beings standing by the throne. KJV "host" leaves it open, as the Hebrew does. | KJV, ESV, NASB 1995 "host(s)" keep the range. NASB 2020 narrows it; NIV "vast array," NRSV "multitude," JPS "array" drop the military sense. |
| 16 | שָׁבַת shavat | Gen 2:2–3; 8:22; Ex 31:17; Josh 5:12 | To cease, stop, leave off. The root of shabbat. Repose is a different verb, nuach (Ex 20:11 uses it of God; Ex 31:17 has both: "he ceased and was refreshed"). | "rested" | The seventh day as recovery from effort, and the Sabbath as a day for rest in the sense of inactivity and recuperation. Gen 2:2–3 says God stopped; it says nothing about fatigue. The Sabbath command then means "cease from work," which is what Ex 20:10 and Deut 5:14 spell out. "Rest" is defensible English for stopping, but it pulls in Ex 20:11's other verb and the idea of tiredness, which the Genesis text does not contain. | JPS "ceased." All Christian versions "rested." |
| 17 | יהוה YHWH | Gen 2:4 (first of \~6,800 occurrences) | A proper name, four consonants. The MT prints it with the vowels of Adonai ("my Lord") as an instruction to say that word aloud, so no manuscript records the name's own vowels. Elohim ("God") is a common noun; YHWH is not. In Gen 2–3 the two stand together, "YHWH God," twenty times. | "the LORD" in small capitals (and "JEHOVAH" four times, e.g. Ex 6:3, Ps 83:18, a hybrid of the consonants with the Adonai vowels) | English readers take "the LORD" as a title, not a name, and "Lord" as a synonym for "God." Two effects: the reader cannot see when the text uses the name and when it uses the common noun (Gen 1 has only Elohim; 2:4 onward has YHWH Elohim), and the hybrid "Jehovah" came to be taken as the name itself. The small-capital convention preserves the Jewish spoken practice, not the written text. | None of the six prints the name; all use "the LORD." The log prints YHWH. |
| 18 | גַּן־בְּעֵדֶן gan-be-Eden | Gen 2:8, 10, 15; 3:23–24 | A garden planted inside a region named Eden, on the ground, watered by a river that feeds the Tigris and Euphrates, described in the present tense with directions (east of Asshur). The human is made outside it and brought in. The text treats it as a place on the map. | "garden" (Genesis). The word "paradise" enters KJV only in the New Testament (Luke 23:43, 2 Cor 12:4, Rev 2:7), carried over from the Greek rendering of 2:8. | Eden as a heavenly or otherworldly place, interchangeable with heaven. KJV's Genesis is faithful; the belief arrived by another route. The Greek translators used paradeisos (a Persian walled park) for the garden; the New Testament uses that word for the place of the blessed dead; KJV transliterates it there as "paradise." English readers meet "paradise" as heaven and carry it back to Eden. In the Hebrew, Eden is earthly, geographic and lost, not above. | All keep "garden" in Genesis. |
| 19 | דַּעַת טוֹב וָרָע da'at tov va-ra | Gen 2:9, 17; 3:5, 22; Deut 1:39; 2 Sam 19:35; Isa 7:15–16 | "Knowing of good and bad." ra is "bad" broadly: harmful, worthless, calamitous, wicked. Outside Genesis the phrase names a faculty: what small children lack (Deut 1:39), the very old lose (2 Sam 19:35), and a child grows into (Isa 7:15–16). It is the capacity to judge, to tell beneficial from harmful. | "knowledge of good and evil" | "Evil" in modern English is moral and absolute, so the tree reads as moral knowledge, or knowledge of sin. The Hebrew pair is wider and, in its other uses, developmental: the thing an adult has and a child does not. KJV's "evil" was broader in 1611 (an "evil" smell, an "evil" day) and has narrowed. The Hebrew word has not. | JPS: "good and bad." The other five keep "good and evil." |
| 20 | לְעָבְדָהּ וּלְשָׁמְרָהּ le-ovdah u-le-shomrah | Gen 2:15; Num 3:7–8, 8:26, 18:5–6 | "To work/serve it and to keep/guard it." avad is the verb for serving a master and for worship; shamar is to guard, the cherubim's verb in 3:24. Together they are the formula for the Levites' duty in the sanctuary. | "to dress it and to keep it" | The human as gardener and groundskeeper. "Dress" is horticultural English and "keep" reads as maintenance, so the sanctuary echo is inaudible; a reader cannot see that the same two verbs describe the Levites. The link between garden and temple, which the Hebrew carries in its vocabulary, is lost in every English version, not only KJV. | None shows it; the verbs are translated differently in Genesis and Numbers in all six. |
| 21 | מוֹת תָּמוּת mot tamut | Gen 2:17; 20:7; Ex 19:12; 1 Sam 14:44; 1 Kgs 2:37, 42 | "Dying you shall die": the stressed verb form, and the standard wording of a death sentence. In 1 Kgs 2:37 the same sentence, with the same "on the day," is pronounced on Shimei; he is executed later, not that day. The idiom declares the penalty, not its timing. | "in the day that thou eatest thereof thou shalt surely die" | KJV is exact. "In the day" permits a clock reading; since the human lives on, that reading requires a death of some other kind on the same day. The Hebrew idiom, used of Shimei, already allows a sentence pronounced now and carried out later; the chapter's own use of yom in 2:4 for the whole creation shows the word is not a 24-hour clock here either. Nothing in the text requires a same-day death of any kind. | All keep "surely die." |
| 22 | עֵזֶר כְּנֶגְדּוֹ ezer kenegdo | Gen 2:18, 20; ezer alone: Ex 18:4; Deut 33:7, 26, 29; Ps 33:20, 121:1–2 | "A helper as his counterpart." ezer is help, most often God's help to Israel; it carries no rank. kenegdo is "like what stands opposite him": a match, a corresponding other. The phrase names an equal and opposite partner. | "an help meet for him" (meet = fitting, suitable) | The woman as subordinate assistant. KJV's phrase was exact in 1611. English then fused "help meet" into a noun, "helpmeet" and "helpmate," a wife whose role is to assist. Two drifts: "help" lost the sense it has when used of God, and "meet" stopped meaning "matching." The Hebrew word for the stronger party's aid became the English word for a junior one. | All six: "helper" with "suitable," "fit," "corresponding to" or "as his partner." |
| 23 | צֵלָע tzela | Gen 2:21–22; Ex 25:12, 26:20; 1 Kgs 6:5; 2 Sam 16:13 | Side. The side of the covenant chest (Ex 25:12), of the tabernacle, of the temple, of a hill. It is never "rib" anywhere else in the Bible. God takes one of the human's sides and builds it (banah, the building verb) into the woman; the human then calls her "bone of my bones," a kinship formula. The picture is a half, not a small part. | "one of his ribs" | "Rib" permits a woman made from a small, spare part of the man; "side" does not. "Rib" came in through the Greek pleura, which means both side and rib, and through later tradition; KJV fixed it in English. The Hebrew word's own usage says "side," and the verb "built" fits a side, not a bone. | All six keep "rib"; several footnote "side." |
| 24 | בָּשָׂר אֶחָד basar echad | Gen 2:24; cf. 2:23, 29:14, 2 Sam 5:1 | "One flesh," where flesh is also "kin" (v.23). The narrator says a man leaves his parents and clings to his woman and "they" become one flesh. The MT has no "two"; LXX, Samaritan, Syriac and the Gospels add it. The sentence is the narrator's comment, not a command, and the man is the one who leaves. | "they shall be one flesh" (Genesis); "they twain shall be one flesh" (Matt 19:5) | The English permits reading the verse as a command and, with "twain" in Matthew, as a statement about number. In Genesis it is an observation about why things are as they are, with habitual verbs. The word "two" that carries the numerical argument is in the Greek and the Gospels, not the Hebrew of Genesis. KJV prints both readings, in two different books, without marking the difference, so an English reader cannot see that Matthew's "twain" is not in Moses. | All six follow the MT ("they"); none notes the Gospel variant in Genesis. |
| 25 | נָחָשׁ nachash | Gen 3:1, 2, 4, 13, 14; Ex 4:3; 7:15; Num 21:6–9; Eccl 10:8, 11; Isa 65:25 | The ordinary word for a snake: the staff that becomes one (Ex 4:3), the biting snakes of the desert (Num 21:6), the snake in a wall (Amos 5:19). 31 uses, all snakes or things compared to snakes (Concordance). 3:1 says it was one of the living-things of the field that YHWH God made, and 3:14 addresses it as an animal. The identification is made in a later book, not in the vocabulary of Gen 3. | All six: "serpent." |
| 26 | עָרוּם arum | Gen 3:1; Job 5:12; 15:5; Prov 12:16, 23; 13:16; 14:8, 15, 18; 22:3; 27:12 | Shrewd, prudent. Spelled with the consonants of "naked" (2:25), so that 2:25 and 3:1 share one word-shape. | "subtil" | "Subtil" in 1611 meant crafty, cunning; "subtle" now means faint, fine, hard to detect. The drift is in English. | NIV, ESV, NASB, NRSV: "crafty"; JPS: "shrewdest." None shows the link to "naked." |
| 27 | כֵּאלֹהִים ke-Elohim | Gen 3:5; cf. 3:22 | "Like Elohim." The word is the one rendered "God" throughout Gen 1–3 and also the plural "gods" (Ex 20:3); the form is the same, and here nothing in the sentence decides it (the plural participle "knowers" agrees with "you"). LXX and Vulgate read "gods." The narrator's counterpart in 3:22 is "like one of us." | "as gods" | Each English choice closes what the Hebrew leaves open. | 28 | הוּא יְשׁוּפְךָ hu yeshufkha / זֶרַע zera | Gen 3:15; zera: 1:11–12, 29; 4:25; 15:5; 21:12 | "He shall strike you \[on the\] head." hu agrees with zera, "seed," a masculine collective singular: the plants' seed of 1:11 and a person's offspring, one (4:25) or many (15:5). "Offspring" (NIV, ESV, NRSV, JPS) loses the link to the plant "seed" of ch. 1, where the word first appears. "Seed": KJV, NASB; "offspring": the other four. |
| 29 | שׁוּף shuf | Gen 3:15 ×2; Job 9:17; Ps 139:11 | To strike, crush. One verb for both blows: the seed strikes the serpent's head, the serpent strikes the seed's heel. Four uses in all; Job 9:17 has God crushing Job with a storm. The verse gives the two parties the same action and different body parts. | "bruise" / "bruise" | KJV keeps one verb. A rendering with two verbs, "crush" for the seed and "strike" for the serpent, permits a reading in which the two blows differ in kind and the outcome is settled by the verbs; the Hebrew settles it, if at all, by "head" and "heel," not by the verb. The asymmetry is the translator's. | KJV, ESV, NASB: "bruise" ×2; NRSV, JPS: "strike" ×2. NIV alone: "crush" / "strike." |
| 30 | עִצָּבוֹן itzavon / עֶצֶב etzev | Gen 3:16 ×2, 17; 5:29; etzev: Prov 5:10; 10:22; 14:23; 15:1; Ps 127:2 | Toil, hard labor, the pain of work. itzavon occurs three times: the woman's (3:16), the man's eating (3:17), and the toil of our hands (5:29). etzev in Proverbs is labor and its wages.
